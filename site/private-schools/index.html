Four questions that determine school choice

State or independent?+

State schools are closed to families without UK residence rights. If you are not relocating, the real question is which independent school fits. If relocation is on the table, we look at both paths.

Boarding or day?+

Boarding means full immersion — the child lives at school, the family is abroad, a guardian is required. Day school works when the family lives in or moves to the UK; the choice follows geography and daily life.

Which stage by age?+

Preparatory schools (ages 7–13) feed the senior schools; senior schools (13–18) lead to GCSE and then A-Level or IB — the programme that shapes university options.

Co-educational or single-sex?+

Co-ed is the natural environment; single-sex remains a strong British tradition, often with higher competition. The answer follows the child's temperament and goals, not a principle.

What a British school costs

Budget reference points, so the decision is made with figures:

  • Day school: roughly £6,000–9,000 per term, depending on city and level
  • Boarding school: £9,500–18,000 per term, tuition and accommodation included
  • Plus: registration fees (£100–300 per application), deposit, uniform, trips

Questions parents ask on the first call

Are state schools open to international families?+

No — state schools are free only for residents. For international families the real choice is the independent system. If relocation is planned, we examine both options.

Can we rely on league tables?+

With caution. A league table shows the quality of a school's intake: selective schools sit higher because they admit the strongest pupils. It does not answer how your child will do there. We look at competition, environment, pastoral care and geography — and only then at tables.

When should we start?+

Comfortably, 24 months before entry; realistically, 12. Registration at the most competitive schools closes two to three years ahead. If your timeline is already short, options remain — the corridor is simply narrower.

Can we do this without a consultant?+

Honestly, yes — applications can be filed directly. The consultant's task is not to fill in a form but to see the whole chain: where the child will pass the competition, how to prepare for a specific school's format, what to say at interview and who the guardian will be afterwards. An error in that chain costs a year.
