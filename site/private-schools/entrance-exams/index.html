Private Schools in England: How the System Works and How to Enter

Entrance Exams for British Private Schools: 11+, 13+, 16+

Which tests your child sits, when and where depends on the entry point and the specific school. ALBION builds preparation from the shortlist, not from a textbook: diagnostics on past papers first, then sessions with tutors who know the format of the specific admissions offices.

The map of entry points

British independent schools have standard entry windows. Each carries its own tests and registration deadline — at the most competitive schools, registration closes two to three years ahead.

7+ / 8+7–8English, maths, interview12–18 months12–18 months
11+10–11ISEB Pre-Test or CAT4 + school papers18–24 months12–18 months
13+12–13ISEB Pre-Test → Common Entrance / CASE24–36 months18–24 months

For international pupils, most schools also require UKiset — covered separately below.

The tests, one by one

ISEB Common Pre-Tests — the online screening for 11+ and 13+: English, maths, verbal and non-verbal reasoning. Adaptive format: questions adjust to the level of the answers. Taken once; the result is read by every school on the list.

Common Entrance (11+ and 13+) — the main written subject exams. At 13+ this is the final gate for most boarding schools: Pre-Test first, CE after. For scholarship places there is CASE, the harder version of 13+.

CAT4 — a cognitive-abilities test: verbal, non-verbal, quantitative and spatial reasoning. Used both for entry and for streaming inside the school. A similar role at some schools is played by CEM Select and Quest — adaptive tests of the same family; we confirm the set for each school on the shortlist.

UKiset — the adaptive test required of most international applicants: academic potential plus English. Its practical value: one profile works for several schools, and schools read it before your documents. A strong UKiset opens the shortlist before school exams begin.

School papers at 16+ — set individually by each school: tests in chosen A-Level subjects plus interview. Here the preparation targets not "the exam" but the specific school's paper.

How we prepare at ALBION

A child does not sit "exams in general" — they sit specific tests for specific schools. Preparation therefore starts after the shortlist:

  1. Academic assessment. Past papers and sessions with our teachers — showing where the child stands against British peers and whether the listed schools are realistic.
  2. A plan by subject. What to strengthen, in what order, with which tutors — against the requirements of the chosen schools.
  3. Sessions. A team of 150+ tutors, including entrance-exam specialists. Subject tutor — from £65/hr; admissions specialist — £85–110/hr; senior specialist — £95–150/hr. More about tutors
  4. Mock exams. A full run-through in the target school's format — the child walks into a familiar procedure, not the unknown.
  5. Practice interviews. We train answers, not scripts — with recorded debriefs.

Preparation and admissions strategy sit in the same hands: the tutor sees the school's requirements, the consultant sees the real trajectory. A split arrangement — consultant plus third-party tutors — makes that synchronisation much harder.

Deadlines that cannot move

  • 13+ (the most competitive point): registration at top schools closes 2–3 years ahead; Pre-Test usually in Year 6, CE in Year 8.
  • 11+: registration a year to eighteen months out; testing in the autumn–winter before entry.
  • 16+: registration in summer–autumn before entry; exams and interviews in autumn.
  • UKiset: can be taken at any time — the profile remains valid and is sent to schools as they are ready.

The rule is simple: the more competitive the school, the earlier registration closes. If your year of entry is known, we will lay out your calendar date by date at the consultation.

Questions

Does every international pupil need UKiset?+

Most, but not all — the requirement varies by school. We check it for every school on the shortlist at the outset, so nothing unnecessary is sat and nothing mandatory is missed.

What if the child fails an exam?+

That is exactly why the list holds five schools at different competition levels — the strategy has a reserve line. UKiset can be retaken; school papers move to the next window if it is still open.

When should preparation start?+

Comfortably 12–24 months, depending on the entry point. Six-to-twelve-month scenarios exist, but then we work with the schools whose windows are open. Even with three years to go, an assessment now shows what to strengthen without haste.

Can it be done in three months?+

It depends on the gap between the current level and the school's requirements. An honest answer comes from diagnostics: sometimes three months is enough; sometimes it is a sign the shortlist needs changing.

What does preparation cost?+

Tuition from £65/hour; the volume follows the assessment — we name an honest number of hours rather than selling a package.

Are the exams taken online or in our country?+

Pre-Test, CAT4 and UKiset are taken online or at accredited centres — there is no need to travel to Britain for the first stage. School papers and interviews at the senior entry points are often in person, sometimes by video.

What if the child is afraid of exams?+

A common and solvable problem: mock runs remove the fear of the unknown, and the tutor works on confidence as well as academics. For anxious profiles we choose a gentler format — without losing the result.
