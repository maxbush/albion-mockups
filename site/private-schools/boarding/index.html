Boarding Schools in the UK: Selection and Admission

A boarding school is school, home and educational environment in one — the choice shapes the next decade of a child's life. ALBION selects British boarding schools to fit the pupil's profile and runs the admission end to end: assessment, entrance exams, documents, interviews and settling in — one team, based in Oxford.

Which school will suit your child

There are more than 500 independent boarding schools in Britain, and the one at the top of a league table may be wrong in temperament, pace and pastoral support. In our experience, a school must be within reach of the entry competition and comfortable once the child arrives — otherwise an offer turns into two difficult years.

The cards below are examples across segments; the full shortlist is built for the individual pupil after assessment.

  • Highly academic

    high competition, a strong scholarly environment, a route toward Oxbridge and the Russell Group.

  • Balanced co-educational

    solid academics and strong pastoral care; suits most families.

  • Single-sex

    girls' and boys' schools; the model is chosen on temperament and goals, not habit.

  • By region

    boarding schools in and around London, Oxfordshire, Kent and the counties: geography drives logistics, guardianship and the exeats — the weekends when the school closes and the child goes to the guardian.

School size, the mix of British and international pupils, the quality of boarding houses — these are checked on visits and through information that never reaches the brochures.

In practice. A family came with a familiar shortlist of famous names. We added an academically outstanding school that international families rarely know. After the visits they chose exactly that one — the pupil went on to strong GCSE and A-Level results. A league table reflects the quality of the intake, not what your child will become in five years.

Entry points: 7+, 11+, 13+, 16+

Boarding schools have standard entry windows — each with its own competition, tests and registration deadline.

Entry pointAgeAssessmentWhen to start
7+ / 8+7–8English, maths, interview12–18 months ahead
11+10–11ISEB Pre-Test or CAT4 + school papers18–24 months
13+12–13ISEB Pre-Test → Common Entrance24–36 months
16+15–16subject papers for A-Level + interview12–18 months

Most schools additionally require UKiset from international pupils — an online measure of academic potential and English, read before your documents are opened.

Missing a window is common: the most competitive schools close registration two to three years out. If your timeline is shorter, we will tell you honestly which options remain open and which have closed.

What boarding school costs

Two budgets, stated openly:

School fees (per term, tuition + boarding):

  • ages 8–13 — roughly £9,500–15,000
  • ages 11–18 — £13,000–18,000 at most schools, higher at the most competitive

Plus uniform, trips, registration fees (£100–300 per school) and deposit.

The admission process, six steps with one team

  1. Academic assessment. Past papers and sessions with our teachers show where the child stands against British peers — and whether the target level is realistic. If the picture is uncomfortable, we say so here, not after a contract.
  2. Shortlist. Up to ten schools, each justified: competition, environment, geography. The final list is usually five.
  3. Preparation. Sessions with ALBION tutors — more than 150 of them, selected by a dedicated specialist — covering subject gaps, exam format, mock testing and interview practice with feedback.
  4. Applications. Registration, forms, references, negotiation with schools on your behalf, deadline control.
  5. Exams and interviews. Coordinating dates and preparing for each school's specific format.
  6. Offer and beyond. Choosing between offers, deposit, Child Student visa, guardianship, settling in across the first terms.

Preparation and strategy sit in the same hands — the tutor sees the admissions goal, the consultant sees the real progress. When strategy lives in one company and preparation in another, that synchronisation is much harder to achieve.

In practice — anonymised. A pupil with a strong sporting profile applied at 13+ in 2022, received offers from every school on the agreed list and chose. Two years later he was captain of the school's rowing team.

Guardianship for international pupils

A British school requires every international pupil under 18 to have an official guardian in the country — the adult who handles daily life, travel, exeats and the unexpected, from a toothache to a closed airport terminal. The quality of guardianship defines the child's everyday life; it is not a formality.

ALBION's guardianship is built differently from contracted agencies: the child is looked after by the consultant who knows them from admission — academic profile, settling-in history, agreements with the school. Places are limited and the service is offered primarily to our own pupils.

Parents' questions

Is boarding too early? What about "boarding school syndrome"?+

An honest answer: boarding does not suit everyone. The child needs basic independence and readiness for communal life — that is what we look at during assessment. For children who find separation hard, there are day schools and weekly boarding — home at weekends. The syndrome is real, but it is addressed by choosing the right environment and strong pastoral care, not by abandoning the idea.

Co-educational or single-sex?+

Both models have arguments. Single-sex schools are historically stronger academically in the UK; co-ed is closer to real life. We decide on the child's temperament and the target university, not on principle.

What if the child does not get in?+

That is why the list has five schools, not one: competition levels within a list differ, and every strategy has a reserve line. 96% of our pupils received a place from the agreed list — but no one can guarantee a specific school, and anyone who promises one is not being straight with you.

A child with special needs (dyslexia, ADHD) — will schools take them?+

Yes. Many schools have strong learning-support departments, and some specialise. We have worked with complex profiles, including neurodiversity — selection there is more precise, but the range of options is wider than families expect. Case: a home-schooled pupil (tournament chess player), academically underdeveloped English, suspected dyslexia — our tutors taught the whole GCSE programme; offer from a strong school for A-Level at 14.

When to start?+

Comfortably — 24 months before entry. Realistically — 12. Faster scenarios exist (spare places, late windows), but the choice narrows. Even with three years to go, a consultation now is cheaper than haste later.

Do you work only with top schools?+

No. A famous name is not the goal — the goal is a place where the child will grow and from which the right university opens. Our lists include both Eton-level schools and strong middle-tier ones — sometimes the second is the better answer.
