Summer schools

Summer Schools in England

A British summer school, chosen well, is not &quot;a holiday with English lessons&quot;. In the right scenario it is a reconnaissance of the British system: two to four weeks inside a real school or college show a child and their parents what no prospectus can — whether boarding will suit them, whether they like the format, and what to strengthen before a real application.

The four types of programme

  • Academic summer schools

    subject programmes in schools and colleges, for families considering admission. Example from our portfolio: Dukes Cambridge (ages 14–17) — university-preparation faculties: medicine (with Medic Portal), STEM, art and design, English pathway; two-week courses from late June.

  • Language programmes

    intensive English plus immersion; for a first encounter with the system.

  • Day camps with English

    for younger children (ages 6–12): activity programmes alongside British children — typically ~85% local English-speaking pupils — with two English lessons a day. Locations include Oxford, London, Reading and Berkshire; from £595 per week.

  • Specialist programmes

    career and professional immersion, e.g. InvestIN (ages 15–18): medicine, law, engineering, investment banking — career testing through practice.

How to choose — what separates a real programme from a brochure

Hundreds of summer programmes are sold to international families, and the difference between a centuries-old school and a commercial camp with a beautiful website is enormous. We select on:

  • composition

    how many British pupils versus an "international reserve"; integration is the point of the exercise;

  • teaching

    who delivers the academic side;

  • content

    subject substance versus activity padding;

  • honest age and level control

    small groups (in our day-camp programmes classes are capped at 12, grouped by level).

School-based programmes

Programmes hosted at real British schools carry their facilities and standards: for example Dulwich College (ages 12–14) and Earlscliffe School (ages 13–17, summer and spring courses) — boarding settings with their own academic frames. Programme details →

Cost and timing

  • Day camps: from £595/week (tuition week; ages 6–12).
  • Booking: January–April; the most sought-after programmes fill early.
  • Guardianship and transfers sit on our side — a summer pupil under 18 travelling alone is covered on the same contour as our school pupils.

From practice

Families use summer as reconnaissance: the child comes home with a clear "my thing / not my thing", and we get an objective picture for the admissions plan. Several of our pupils came to boarding school exactly after a summer test-drive.

Questions

At what age does it make sense?+

From 6–7 in day formats with a parent nearby; residential academic programmes — from 11–12, the university-prep tier from 14.

Will two weeks change the English?+

Language gains are real but modest; the bigger yield is motivation and the honest read on readiness for school abroad.

Summer school or language school year-round?+
