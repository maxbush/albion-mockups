Tutoring

Private tutoring for GCSE, A-Level, IB and entrance exams

Tutoring can be aimed at very different things: a school offer, a predicted grade, a university test, a gap that has opened in one subject. At ALBION the first conversation is about which of these it is, and the tutor is chosen from the answer rather than from a list of profiles.

More than 150 tutors work with the company from different time zones, and between them they teach over 3,000 hours a year. A lesson with a subject specialist can be set at an hour that suits your family, wherever you live.

Where to start

  • GCSE and IGCSE tutors: subjects, exam boards and how a preparation plan is built, for Years 10 and 11 and for pupils moving into the British system.
  • A-Level tutors: choosing subjects with a degree in mind, predicted grades and exam preparation.
  • IB tutors: the Diploma Programme, Extended Essay, TOK and CAS.
  • Revision courses: structured holiday preparation before exam season, including Easter revision.
  • Homeschooling with our tutors: for a child studying outside a school, with the whole timetable built around academic and non-academic needs.

If the target is a place rather than a grade, see entrance exams for 11+, 13+ and 16+ and university admissions tests.

Online tutors for maths, English and the sciences

Online tutoring for maths begins with a diagnostic lesson, so that the tutor can tell a gap in one topic from a wider problem with fluency. An online maths tutor and an online English tutor work in the same way: one to one, at an hour set around your family, with the plan reviewed as the lessons go on. English is checked first for every pupil, which means that for some children an online English tutor is where the work starts, before maths or the sciences. Whether the aim is GCSE, A-Level or IB, mathematics online tutoring starts the same way, and the sciences follow the same method.

How a tutor is matched

English is checked first. A pupil who struggles to read the question will under-perform in every subject, and the results then mean little. After that a tutor gives a one-to-one online lesson in each chosen subject and assesses knowledge, skills and potential at the next level, set against British peers at independent schools.

You then receive a written plan: which gaps come first, how often to meet, which exam or entrance test the work is aimed at. We do not sell hour packages in advance, because the scope comes from the assessment.

Every tutor is personally selected by Jonathan Mintram (BA Hons, CELTA), and your consultant explains why a particular person is being recommended.

Formats and hourly rates

Lessons run online, one to one, in your time zone. In Oxford they can also be held in person, for families who live in or visit the UK. Tutors can travel to a family anywhere in the world, during holidays or term time.

When tutoring is part of an application

Where lessons belong to an admissions plan, the consultant can see how they are going, and the tutor knows which school or course the pupil is aiming for and what the exam will look like. A change of plan then takes one conversation, not two companies.

A boy at an international school abroad was due to move to a British boarding school in Year 10. By the time his family came to ALBION the usual application window had closed, his English was still developing and several subjects had gaps. Instead of a rushed application, the consultants proposed nearly a year of lessons with our tutors in English, maths and the sciences. Applications then went to selective schools outside the normal cycle.

His family's first-choice school declined Year 10, so the consultants asked it to consider Year 9 instead, which suited a pupil still adjusting to a new language and system. It agreed. He did not get the Year 10 entry the family had asked for, but he took the place and gained a year to build a solid foundation.

What tutoring will not solve

Conversational English alone is a different service, covered under language preparation. And if a goal looks unrealistic for the time available, you will hear that at the start, not three months in.

Questions and answers

How many lessons will my child need?+

That depends on the diagnostic. One or two gaps before an exam can be closed in a short run of lessons, while a pupil moving across from another education system may need a year to level up.

How is ALBION different from a tutoring agency or an online marketplace?+

A marketplace hands you a catalogue to choose from. Here the diagnostic comes first, and the tutor is matched to its findings rather than to whoever has a free slot. If your child is applying to a school or university, the lessons are also tied to the deadlines and requirements of that application.

What are the best online tutoring platforms for GCSE and A-Level?+

ALBION does not rank platforms, and lists of the "best" mostly measure size or price. A better test is what happens before the first lesson: whether the subject is assessed, who chooses the tutor and on what basis, and whether the tutor knows the exam board and the target.

Do we need to be an admissions client to book tutoring?+

No. Tutoring can be arranged on its own. If you are working with ALBION on admissions, consultants fit the lessons into the wider plan.

What should we have ready for a first conversation?+

Your child's age or year group, the current school or country, the nearest exam or goal, and the subjects that worry you most. These go into the application form, and a consultant then suggests whether to begin with a diagnostic or with preparation for a specific exam.

Can you promise a particular grade?+

No. We are accountable for the diagnostic, the tutor match and the plan. Final results depend on the starting point, the time available and the pupil's own work, none of which we control.

Do you teach adults?+

Yes, where the aim is a university or business school test or a language exam: GMAT, GRE, IELTS, DELF or the Goethe-Zertifikat.
