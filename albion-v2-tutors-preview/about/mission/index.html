About ALBION

Our mission

ALBION's aim is modest: to help a family plan its way through British education instead of reacting to it. That holds for a child preparing for a first school entrance test and for an adult choosing an executive programme, and the same few principles govern both.

Book a consultation

Advice that nobody else pays for

A consultancy is only as candid as its income allows. Families pay us. Schools and universities pay nothing and the firm takes no commission from either, so a shortlist is built around the pupil or applicant and not around an arrangement. The about page has the wider picture of how the company is built.

Understanding comes before applying

British admissions run school by school, each with its own tests, dates and expectations. The consultant's job is to turn that scatter of requirements into a plan: where the child stands now, what has to be decided this year, and what can wait. The family makes the decisions and knows what they rest on. The child, meanwhile, can concentrate on learning and not on paperwork.

Success is not the same for every child

Some pupils arrive needing support, others are aiming at the most competitive schools and universities. For each, a good result looks different, so the target is built around the child and not taken from a list of famous names. The assessment looks at strengths and also at any difficulty that needs attention. Occasionally the outcome differs from the family's first aim, and the results page says so openly.

A fallback before the application, not after

Anfisa Bashkirova came to education consulting from Intel, where she managed risk, and the habit of asking what could go wrong shaped how plans are made. An Oxbridge programme includes a strategy for reserve universities, and school work starts from a shortlist of several places. The answer to "what if they say no" should exist before anything is submitted.

A university is a means

A course is judged by what the student will do with the degree. The beauty of the prospectus and the colour of student life do not count, and entry requirements are compared only afterwards.

What we do not promise

We advise, and the offer of a place comes from the school or university. For that reason admission is never promised, and results are quoted as the outcomes of past years, with caveats. Fees for the university programme are published on the prices page, so a family sees what it is paying for before the first conversation.

If you would like to talk about a specific child, the application form comes first. It asks about the goal, age or year group, timing and country of residence.

Questions and answers

Is ALBION tied to any schools?+

No. We are not an agent for schools and take no commission from them or from universities. Each name on a shortlist comes with a reason for its place there.

Can you help a child who finds school hard?+

Yes. We work with children whose studies are difficult as well as with very able ones. The assessment shows where the gaps are, and a tutor is matched to the actual need, whether that is closing gaps or preparing for a competitive exam. What is realistic for the individual child is said after the assessment.

Must a family use ALBION for the whole route?+

No. The route is one continuous chain, but a family can enter at any stage: choosing a school, preparing for a particular test, applying to university or looking at an MBA. The application form helps decide where to begin.

We live outside the UK. Is that a problem?+

It is not. The tutors work across time zones and can teach anywhere in the world. If a child is under 18 and the parents are abroad, a British school will require an official guardian, which the guardianship page explains.
