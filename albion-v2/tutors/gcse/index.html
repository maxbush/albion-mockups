GCSE and IGCSE tutors for maths, English, sciences and more

GCSE grades are the first results a sixth form will look at, and a university admissions tutor will see them later. Whether the need is maths, English or the sciences, ALBION's GCSE tutors work subject by subject and exam board by exam board. They begin with a diagnostic lesson, build a plan around the specification your child will sit, and practise on past papers until the exam format is familiar.

Who comes for GCSE tutoring

A Year 10 or Year 11 pupil in the UK who needs one or two subjects strengthened before the exams is the most common case. Children at international schools who follow a British curriculum abroad come too. So do families moving into the British system from another country, where gaps against the UK programme need closing before a school move. Homeschooled pupils can have subjects taught by our tutors from start to finish, and those retaking after disappointing results are welcome.

If your child is preparing to enter a school rather than sit GCSEs, see entrance exams for 11+, 13+ and 16+.

Choosing by subject

Maths. The first question is whether your child has a gap in a single topic or a more general problem with fluency. A GCSE maths tutor answers it in the diagnostic lesson, and the plan follows from that. The second is tier: a GCSE mathematics tutor will ask whether the pupil is entered for Foundation or Higher, because the papers and the grades available differ.

English. Language and literature are different papers with different skills: one rewards analysis of unseen texts and clear argument, the other close knowledge of set books. A GCSE English tutor online works on both and starts with reading and writing quality, because everything else depends on it.

Sciences. Biology, chemistry and physics, taught separately or as combined science, depending on what the school offers. A GCSE science tutor goes through the specification topic by topic, so that the paper holds fewer surprises.

Humanities and languages. History, geography, French, Chinese and other languages are available, subject to tutor availability in the season you need. Your consultant confirms who is free after the diagnostic.

Exam boards and grades

AQA, Edexcel, OCR and WJEC set GCSEs for schools in England and Wales. International GCSEs are offered by Cambridge (CIE) and Pearson Edexcel. Questions are worded differently, mark schemes reward different things, and a board's habits do not carry across. A tutor therefore asks which specification the school or exam centre uses before anything else, then prepares on that board's past papers and mark schemes.

Grades in England run from 9 down to 1, with 9 the highest. A standard pass is 4 and a strong pass is 5. Sixth forms and colleges set their own minimum grades and may ask for more in the subject a pupil wants to continue.

GCSE or IGCSE

IGCSE is the international version, designed for schools outside the UK. The two qualifications are close in level but not in content or paper structure, and the differences vary by subject and board. A child in an international school normally sits what the school has chosen. For a homeschooled child the choice depends on age, country of residence, the next school or university, and which exam centres are within reach. Your consultant works through that with you.

An IGCSE tutor follows the same sequence as for GCSE: diagnostic, specification, past papers. The materials differ.

How a plan is built

Diagnostic. English is checked first, then a tutor teaches one lesson per subject and assesses knowledge and skills. Where your child stands is measured against British peers at independent schools.

Gap map. You receive a plan listing which topics come first, how many hours a week are needed and when mock papers are set. If the time left is too short for the stated target, you are told.

Past papers. Timed practice on the correct board, with feedback on where marks are lost: content, method, or the way answers are laid out.

Review. After the mocks the plan is revisited, and hours go up, down or move to another subject.

Online GCSE tutoring, in person and rates

An online GCSE tutor teaches one to one, at a time set around your family's timetable, in term time or in the holidays. For GCSE tuition online the plan, the board's past papers and the mock exams are the same as in person; only the lesson moves to wherever you live. Lessons can also be held in person in Oxford, and tutors can travel to a family anywhere in the world. Tutor levels and rates are on the tutors page, and the full price list sits on its own page.

From our files: a retake that changed the route

A family came to ALBION after GCSE results meant their child could not continue to A-Level at their boarding school. The new school year was under two weeks away, and with those grades the usual route into a sixth form was difficult. Rather than search only for A-Level places, our consultants found a small, supportive school that offered a one-year GCSE retake with close attention to each pupil. The grades improved significantly. The pupil moved on to A-Level, sat the exams well and is now finishing university.

The price of that story was a year, and a year cannot be given back. It is one reason to start lessons before the exams rather than after results day. A retake year also suits some pupils and not others.

What tutors do not do

A tutor does not replace the school, write coursework or set predicted grades. Those come from teachers, and we have no influence over them. Tutors close specific gaps and make the exam format familiar. Where the pupil ends up still depends on the starting point, how much time remains and how consistently they work.

Next in line is A-Level tutoring, which picks up the same approach for the sixth form.

Questions and answers

How many hours a week does GCSE tuition need?+

The diagnostic sets the number, and the plan is reviewed after mock exams. One gap in one topic can be closed in a few sessions. A pupil switching from another system needs a much longer run.

When should we start?+

The earlier the calmer. From Year 10 there is time both for the gaps and for getting used to the format. A few months is enough to close individual topics but too little to rebuild how a pupil prepares. When time is short, a revision course in the holidays packs the preparation into one block, and a consultant will say plainly what result is realistic.

Can my child take GCSE or IGCSE without attending a school?+

Usually yes, as a private candidate through an exam centre, though centre rules and availability vary by country. ALBION tutors prepare your child for the exam, and the centre and dates are something a consultant works through with you before lessons begin.

Will a tutor help if my child's English is weak?+

Yes, though English will need strengthening alongside the subjects. If the diagnostic shows that language is getting in the way, the plan includes English lessons, so that your child understands both the questions and the explanations.

How much does a GCSE maths tutor cost?+

Hourly rates are published and start from £65, rising with the tutor's level, which depends on the goal. Work on a current GCSE syllabus sits at the lower end of the range as a rule. The levels are set out on the tutors page.

Is online GCSE tuition effective?+

It can be, and the screen matters less than how the lessons are run. One to one, a tutor sees where a pupil loses marks and responds within the lesson, and mock papers show whether the plan is working. An online maths tutor for GCSE teaches the same specification as one in the room. Some children concentrate better face to face, so in Oxford lessons can be in person.

Can my child resit GCSE maths?+

Yes. GCSEs can be retaken, at a school or college or as a private candidate, and sixth forms often ask for a pass in maths and English before a pupil moves on. The retake in our case above took a full year, so it is worth planning early. A consultant checks centre rules and dates before lessons start.

Who are the best GCSE tutors?+

ALBION does not publish a ranking. For GCSE the word "best" comes down to fit: someone who knows the specification the school uses, finds the gap in your child's work and teaches in a way the child keeps up with. Every ALBION tutor is selected personally by Jonathan Mintram, and subject, age and exam board drive the match, not whichever slot is free.

Can you tell us what grade our child will get?+

No. We can say where your child stands against British peers and how much work is needed to move closer to the target. A specific grade cannot be promised, because too much of it depends on things outside a tutor's control.
