A-Level tutors: maths, sciences and the grades universities ask for

A-Levels are the qualification universities read most closely. The subjects you take decide which courses are open, predicted grades decide which offers arrive, and final results decide whether those offers hold. ALBION's A-Level tuition teaches the subject itself, whether that calls for a maths tutor, a chemistry tutor or a physics tutor, while consultants check that the lessons fit the application: tests, deadlines, the choice of universities.

Book a consultation

Who it is for

Sixth Form pupils who need to lift one subject before applying are the obvious group. Some are moving to A-Level from another system or an international school and closing gaps against peers who have followed the UK programme for two years, or are aged 18 or over and retaking individual subjects. Students who have already been admitted use lessons to keep pace with their school's standard.

Subjects

Maths and Further Maths. An A-Level maths tutor begins with a diagnostic lesson that shows whether the weakness lies in pure content, in mechanics and statistics, or in how answers are set out. The lessons run online, so an online A-Level maths tutor can be matched wherever the family lives. Where a university test is on the horizon, TMUA preparation sits alongside the course.

Chemistry. An A-Level chemistry tutor works through the specification of your exam board topic by topic and drills the question types that cost marks. Medicine typically asks for chemistry, so results here can decide a whole application.

Physics. An A-Level physics tutor builds problem-solving fluency and the link between theory and mathematics. Engineering and other physics-heavy degrees also lean on A-Level maths, so the two subjects are planned together.

Biology and other subjects. Biology belongs with the natural sciences, and an A-Level biology tutor is matched by exam board and availability like the others. For any further subject, name it and the board in the form, and your consultant confirms who is free after the diagnostic.

A-Level tutors online and in person

Lessons are one-to-one and run online, so a pupil can work with an A-Level tutor online wherever the family lives, with the timetable set around the school day. In-person lessons can be arranged in Oxford or at the family's home. The diagnostic lesson is online too.

Choosing subjects with a degree in mind

Most pupils pick three or four A-Levels, and the combination counts for more than it seems in Year 11. Some courses expect particular subjects, and a few look closely at the mix. A wrong choice shows up a year or two later, when it is hard to put right.

Before any lessons an ALBION consultant sets the intended direction against current results and course requirements, and tutors assess the subject itself: is the maths strong enough for a demanding course, is the physics base solid. When two options are close, the decision rests on evidence, not preference.

Predicted grades, deadlines and tests

Schools enter predicted grades in the UCAS application, and universities make conditional offers on that basis. A tutor does not set them and cannot overrule a teacher. What a tutor can do is strengthen the evidence behind the forecast: steady results in school tests, mock exams and set work through Year 12 and the first half of Year 13. We advise against waiting until spring: by the autumn of Year 13 each subject needs a clear position, so the consultant can judge which university list is realistic.

UCAS applications are due by 15 January, and for Oxford and Cambridge by 15 October. By then a candidate needs results that make the case and often a test as well: LNAT, TARA or TMUA. A-Level tutors supply the subject depth, while entrance-test and interview preparation is led by a specialist or senior specialist.

Applications to Oxford and Cambridge and university admissions tests have their own pages. ALBION reports that three in four of the pupils it works with are invited to interview at Oxford or Cambridge. That figure reflects the whole admissions programme, not lessons alone.

How preparation works

A Sixth Form diagnostic covers the four subjects a pupil is considering. In each, a tutor gives a one-to-one online lesson and assesses knowledge, skills and the potential to study the subject at A-Level. English is checked first, because a language barrier would distort the picture.

A plan follows, with a timetable of lessons. The tutor works from your school's exam board specification, goes through past papers and mark schemes, and shows where marks are lost through presentation rather than ignorance. Timed mocks come before the exams. Sometimes one subject is enough, sometimes steady work across three is needed. In the holidays, revision courses can carry the heaviest load.

From our files: exam adjustments for an A-Level pupil

A pupil aged 18 or over was entering A-Level, and her adjustment needs were known in advance. The ALBION team arranged a specialist assessment (it took persuading the pupil herself, since at 18 nothing can be done without her consent), secured support in her exams and organised an emergency exam in her home language that the school had not wanted to submit.

Extra time is granted by the school on the basis of an assessment, so the outcome cannot be promised in advance. The story shows what support looks like when exam preparation, assessment and dealings with the school sit with one team.

Limits

Tutors do not promise grades. If the chosen subjects do not fit the course a pupil wants, lessons alone will not mend that: the university list has to be rethought, and that is the consultant's job. If too little time remains before the exams, ALBION says what can be closed and what cannot.

Questions and answers

How do I choose A-Level subjects if I have not chosen a university?+

Keep options open and lean on the pupil's strengths. A consultant compares combinations with typical course requirements, and a tutor tests whether the pupil can handle the subject at depth.

When should A-Level tutoring start?+

In the first term of Year 12. Most of the content builds in the first year, and gaps are far easier to close before Year 13. For Oxbridge, with its 15 October deadline, the time is tighter still.

Do final results still matter once an offer has arrived?+

Yes. Predicted grades decide which conditional offer a university makes, and final results decide whether it is confirmed. Preparation therefore does not stop when the application goes in.

Can a pupil outside the UK work with an online A-Level tutor?+

Yes. We need to know which specification and exam board your child will sit, and the diagnostic lesson shows where to begin.

Do students who have already been admitted need a tutor?+

Sometimes. If your child is coping, no. If results in one subject start to fall, a tutor can hold the level while the consultant decides whether the plan needs adjusting.

How much does a private A-Level tutor cost?+

The hourly rate depends on the tutor's level, and the total on how many hours the subject and starting point require. Levels and rates are on the tutors overview, and the price list covers every service. Academic lessons are paid separately from a university admissions programme.

Can you retake A-Levels?+

Yes. Individual subjects can be resat in a later exam series, and students aged 18 or over sometimes do so as private candidates. Whether a university accepts the improved grade depends on the university and the course, so check its policy first. A tutor works on what cost marks the first time: paper technique, mark schemes, thin topics.

Can ALBION find an A-Level economics, history or psychology tutor?+

Our tutors teach across a wide range of academic subjects, but we confirm a tutor for a given subject and exam board only after the short form, not in advance.

Can we start with a single subject?+

Yes. The weakest subject is the logical place to begin, with others added after the first results.
