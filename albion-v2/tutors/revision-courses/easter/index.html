Revision Courses for GCSE, A-Level and IB

Easter Revision Courses for A-Level and GCSE

Mock results usually arrive with a list of things that went wrong, and the Easter holiday is the last long stretch in which to deal with them before the May and June papers. With lessons paused, there is time to find where marks are being lost and to put that right. ALBION tutors spend that time on exam technique: timed past papers, marking against the mark scheme and a close look at every error.

Book a consultation

Where marks are lost

British papers award marks against criteria, so knowing the material accounts for only part of the result. In maths, the method earns marks as well as the answer, which means working should be set out so that it still scores if the last line goes wrong. In English and the humanities, assessment objectives decide what a question tests and what a response has to show. In the sciences and economics, command words such as explain, evaluate and discuss call for different depths of answer.

Most of a tutor's time goes here. A topic the student already understands is not taught again. The task is to turn that understanding into marks.

Inside a course

The tutor begins with recent mock papers and school work, and picks the topics likely to return the most marks. Full past papers from the student's own exam board, whether AQA, Edexcel, OCR or another, are then sat under timed conditions and marked against the published scheme.

Every lost mark is given a cause: a gap in knowledge, a slip in calculation, a misread command word, time pressure or a loosely built answer. The cause shows what needs teaching and what only needs practice. Weak topics return in shorter tasks until the result holds.

Who it suits, and who should wait

A course suits a student who knows the content but sees gaps in mock results. These show up as answers that run out of time, marks lost on wording though the topic is understood, and errors that repeat from paper to paper. It also suits a student aiming higher, for example from a B to an A or from an A to an A*. It is equally useful for a candidate sitting British exams outside a UK school who wants to test preparation against exam board format.

It suits a student less when the basics of a subject are missing and the exam is weeks away. A holiday course cannot rebuild a syllabus. After the diagnosis we say so, and suggest either regular lessons with an A-Level tutor or a GCSE tutor, or a narrower aim for the course.

The 2026 course: A-Level Maths Exam Mastery

The 2026 Easter course was built for A-Level Mathematics: two weeks (30 March to 3 April and 6 to 10 April), ten hours of teaching a week, taught online in small groups. It was meant for Year 12 and Year 13 students preparing for final papers on any of the main UK exam boards. In the second week students sat a timed mock exam, and every week they received written feedback.

Students were placed in one of two tracks. The first was for stronger candidates, with hard questions and detailed work on how to take the most marks from higher-level papers. The second explained topics more fully, with worked examples and structured notes, before moving on to exam-style questions. Each day had two sessions: topic review and technique first, then past papers. The first week followed the topics (algebra and functions, calculus, trigonometry, statistics, mechanics), and the second was given to mixed problems, timed work and the mock.

Before a place is confirmed we usually speak with the family about the student's level, exam board, difficult topics and revision priorities. That conversation shows whether the course fits and which track is better. Dates for the next intake are published once confirmed, and subjects beyond maths are confirmed at booking.

What the Easter course costs

The price of a course depends on its format, the number of hours and the level of tutor, and we give it after a first conversation, once the right programme is clear. For orientation, lessons with an academic tutor start at £65 an hour. The prices page sets out the other levels.

What we do not promise

We cannot promise a grade. A course improves the odds through technique and error analysis, but it does not stand in for a year of work. If the diagnosis shows that a target is out of reach, we say so before the course begins. For the difference between a revision course and regular tuition, and for which holidays suit each, see our overview of revision courses.

Questions and answers

When should we apply?+

Early. Diagnosis and tutor matching take time, and the Easter break is short. The form can be completed before dates are published, and we contact the family once a course is confirmed.

Does it cover both GCSE and A-Level?+

The 2026 course was devoted to A-Level Mathematics. For GCSE and other subjects we agree the programme at booking: GCSE leans towards precise wording and secure basics, A-Level towards depth and argument, and the tutor is matched to subject and level.

Do we need to know the exam board?+

It helps, and it is normally printed in school documents. If you are unsure, we can identify it from the most recent mock paper.

Is it one-to-one or in a group?+

The 2026 course ran online in small groups. One-to-one lessons with an ALBION tutor run through the year, at the rates on the prices page, and we confirm the format of the next intake with its dates.

Can families outside the UK take part?+

Yes, if the student is sitting British exams, for instance IGCSE or A-Level at an international school. Lessons are online. Term dates abroad may not match the British ones, and we discuss that at booking.

How many hours will a student need?+

The 2026 course ran for 20 hours over two weeks, ten a week. For one-to-one preparation the diagnosis decides, since it depends on the subjects and the size of the gaps, and we do not quote a range in advance.
