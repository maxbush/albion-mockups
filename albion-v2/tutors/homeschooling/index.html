Homeschooling with our tutors

A family that educates at home needs two things from a tutor: a plan that fits the child, and someone accountable for it. ALBION arranges lessons in the school holidays or in term time, wherever the family lives, and builds the week around the child's academic needs and everything else in it, from sport to travel.

Book a consultation

Who chooses this format

The reasons differ. Frequent moves between countries, a training or performance schedule that a school timetable cannot hold, gaps left by a change of system, or GCSE, A-Level or IB preparation outside a classroom. In each case the timetable has to bend around the child, not the other way round.

Some families bring in a tutor for the holidays only, to keep momentum. Others organise the whole school year around one-to-one lessons. The scope is agreed at the start.

How the lessons are organised

The first step is an academic assessment, which our teachers run online on past papers. The result is set against UK peers and shows both the gaps and a realistic outlook before any hours are booked. A written academic plan follows: subjects, weekly load, exams and dates. Where the aim is a UK qualification, the work follows GCSE, A-Level or IB content.

We have more than 150 tutors across different time zones, and Jonathan Mintram selects each of them personally. For a family on another continent that range makes a workable lesson time possible. Whether a tutor travels or lessons run another way is settled during matching. Subjects and stages are covered more broadly on the tutors page.

Rates

Home education is charged at the tutor's hourly rate. The level is settled at the start and depends on the goal, the difficulty of the work and which specialists are available.

An academic tutor raises results in a subject and prepares for exams. A specialist or senior specialist is brought in when subject depth or experience of competitive preparation is needed. Entrance exam and interview preparation costs more, and those rates are on the prices page. The total follows hours per week and the length of the programme.

What this service does not replace

In the UK the responsibility for a child's education rests with the parents, who may meet it through a school or at home. That is the general position only. The rules that apply to your family depend on where you live, and we do not give legal advice. Lessons are built to match the qualification the child is working towards. We are not a school and do not take over legal responsibility for education, and no grade can be promised: results depend on the starting level and on regular attendance.

Questions and answers

Can a tutor travel to us?+

Travelling is part of the service, and tutors are prepared to work where the family lives. Travel, schedule and format are agreed during matching, so give your country and preferred dates in the questionnaire.

Can we begin with the holidays only?+

Yes. Lessons run in the holidays or in term time, and you choose where to start.

Does a child already on a UK curriculum need an assessment?+

It saves time. The assessment shows the gaps and a candid outlook, so the first lessons are not spent working out the level.

Will you prepare my child for a return to school?+

If that is the aim, yes: closing gaps and preparing for entrance exams into a UK school. The lesson plan is agreed with our school advisers so that it fits the admissions timetable.

Give your country, age and dates in the questionnaire
