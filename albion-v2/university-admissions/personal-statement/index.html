What Is a Personal Statement? How to Write One for Law and Medicine

A personal statement is a short piece of writing in which an applicant explains why they want a place on a course and what they have done to prepare for it. Grades are what they are by the time of applying, but the statement shows an admissions tutor what the applicant has read, thought about and done beyond the syllabus. On competitive courses, above all law and medicine, many candidates arrive with similar results, and the statement can decide who is invited to interview. At ALBION it is handled as part of the wider admissions plan, from positioning for the faculty to consistency with the school reference.

What tutors look for

The reader is a subject tutor asking whether this person will use three years of teaching well. The strongest statements show a mind at work: a book that changed an opinion, a question the applicant pursued, an idea tested in practice and what came of it. A personal statement for university entry is written for a subject, not for one institution, because a single text goes to every course on the application.

The same few weaknesses turn up again and again. A life story told in order, where an argument should be. A list of achievements with no sentence saying why they matter for the course. A motive that persuades the family but not a stranger: if the answer to "why law?" is future earnings, the choice looks unconsidered.

UCAS personal statement questions and length

For entry from 2026 onwards, UCAS replaced the single free-form text with three questions. They ask why you want to study the course or subject, how your qualifications and studies have prepared you for it, and what else you have done outside education and why those experiences are useful. For the 2027 cycle, UCAS sets a total of 4,000 characters including spaces, with at least 350 characters for each answer. How the space is divided is up to the applicant, and admissions staff read the three answers as one text, so evidence should not be repeated.

The limit is set in characters, not words, so any figure in words is only an estimate: the counter in the UCAS form decides. Guidance written for earlier years may describe a different format, which is why the consultant checks the requirements for your year of entry before the first draft. The rest of the application, including deadlines and the reference, is covered on the UCAS application support page.

How to write a personal statement

Begin with material, not sentences. List what the applicant has read, studied and done, and beside each item write what it showed them about the subject. Anything that produces no such sentence can usually be dropped. Each paragraph of the draft is then tested against one question: what does this tell the tutor about how this person will cope with the course?

Expect several drafts: the cuts matter as much as the additions. Reading aloud helps, as does a reader who will say where it fails to convince.

How to start a personal statement

The opening is easiest to write last. A natural way in is the answer to the first question: a specific book, problem, case or observation that made the subject real, and what the applicant did next. Quotations and the stock line about wanting to be a doctor or lawyer since childhood are best avoided: a tutor will have met them before.

UCAS personal statement examples: how to read them

Published personal statement examples for university entry are useful as an illustration of structure. They show how a paragraph moves from an experience to a point about the course, and how a claim sits next to its evidence. They mislead when treated as a template. A statement is written for one person and one subject, and an example from an earlier year was composed for a different format. Borrowed phrasing also tends to give itself away, and at interview the applicant can be asked about any claim.

How we work on the statement

A consultant and a subject mentor work on the statement together. The raw material comes from the applicant: what has been read, what has been done, where there is an opinion of their own. Positioning for the faculty then decides what to bring forward for the courses on the list and what to leave out, which belongs to stage 2 of the programme.

The first draft is the student's. After that the text goes through several rounds of revision. The consultant and mentor point out where the argument is thin, where a fact does not support the conclusion and where the text drifts from the shortlist. The Oxbridge track has more rounds, because the statement is read especially closely there. ALBION does not write the school reference or alter predicted grades, but it checks that the statement does not contradict them, and coordination with the school is part of stage 3.

The voice belongs to the student, and an adult's prose reads differently to a tutor. The consultants help to find material, shape an argument and cut what does not earn its place. The sentences are the applicant's.

Timing and fees

Material for a statement builds up long before submission: reading, olympiads, essay contests, experience in the field. With only a few months left, the work proceeds from what already exists, because interest cannot be grown in a short time, only shown better.

There is no separate "statement only" fee. Work on the statement sits in stage 3, Application preparation (£2,500; £3,000 on the Oxbridge track), and draws on stages 1 and 2. Families who join later can take stage 3 alone, but the starting point is always a consultation with a written report, at £350 or £500 for Oxbridge. The full programme costs £6,000, or £9,500 for Oxbridge. The admissions overview sets out each stage, the prices page gathers the rates, and the extra steps for Oxford and Cambridge are described in the Oxbridge section.

Questions and answers

How long should a personal statement be?+

Within the UCAS limit of 4,000 characters, across three answers of at least 350 characters each. How the space is shared out depends on the course and on where the strongest evidence lies.

How many characters is a personal statement, and how many words?+

Spaces count towards the 4,000. UCAS gives the limit in characters, so there is no official answer to how many words in a UCAS personal statement. Work to the character counter in the form.

How do I type a personal statement for UCAS?+

Draft each answer separately in a plain document, with a character count that includes spaces, so that each can be pasted into its own box. After pasting, check the counter in the UCAS form and reread the text as it appears there.

How is the UCAS personal statement different from a master's motivation letter?+

The UCAS statement is written for undergraduate entry, and one text goes to every course. For a master's, you apply directly to universities and write for a specific programme: see master's admissions.

What is a personal statement example good for?+

Studying structure. Copying phrasing is a risk: UCAS checks statements for similarity with other texts, and a tutor can tell from the language when the applicant is not the one speaking.

Will ALBION write my statement for me?+

No. You write the drafts, and the consultant and mentor return them with comments. We help build the argument and the positioning; the content and the voice stay yours.

Does one personal statement work for all five courses?+

Yes, UCAS takes a single statement for every choice, up to five. That is why the courses on the shortlist should be related, or a shared text will not persuade any panel. The shortlist is built in stage 1.
