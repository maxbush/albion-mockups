UK University Admissions

A UCAS application has five choices and one personal statement, and most of the decisions that matter are made before either is written. ALBION advises students and families on the whole sequence: the profile, the course shortlist, the application, admissions tests, interviews and the choice between offers. The page is written for parents of children in the last years of GCSE, A-Level or IB, and for students applying independently who would like their plan read by someone who knows how admissions tutors read.

How a UCAS application works

UCAS is the single route for undergraduate applications to UK universities. Up to five choices go on one form, and all five receive the same personal statement, the same school reference and the same predicted grades. The courses should therefore be related: one text cannot persuade five panels about five unrelated subjects. Submission and deadlines are covered on the UCAS support page.

Universities reply with offers. A conditional offer depends on final A-Level or IB results, an unconditional one does not. The applicant names a firm choice and an insurance choice, and if results fall short of the plan, Clearing and Adjustment remain.

Oxford and Cambridge follow a separate track. An applicant may apply to only one of them, the deadline is earlier, many courses require an admissions test, and interviews are held in December. Our Oxbridge page covers that track in detail.

Choosing a course for the long term

League tables, an attractive campus and the university a friend attended make a poor basis for a decision that shapes the next ten years. We ask who the student is likely to be at thirty and which degree leads there, so a course is judged by where it goes after graduation as well as by how the offer looks on the day.

That is why the work starts with the academic profile: subject combination, exam results, predicted grades and the profession in mind. A pupil can be a strong candidate for one course and a weak one for another with a similar name. A consultant's job includes saying which is which.

When to start

The point of entry decides what can still be changed. Two to four years out, a family controls subject choice, reading, competitions and essay contests. A year out, the statement, tests and course list are what remain. With a few months to go, subjects are fixed and predicted grades exist, so the work narrows to the application and interview practice.

The full programme is designed for families who begin early, since it runs the whole campaign through to the decision on offers. Those who arrive later can buy individual stages.

  • Personal statement: for applicants to law and medicine, where the text is read with particular care.
  • Admissions tests: LNAT, TMUA, UCAT, MAT and others, when a course requires one.

  • US universities and universities in Europe: different systems, different deadlines.
  • Open days: planning visits to universities and colleges.

What ALBION does not promise

No adviser can promise an offer, because the admissions panel decides. The record so far is that every client has ended up with a Russell Group offer in hand (100%). It reflects work on individual profiles and is not a forecast for the next family.

ALBION takes no commission from universities, so the course list comes from the student's profile and nothing else. The tutors who prepare students for tests and interviews work alongside the consultant, which keeps the lessons tied to one plan. Admissions for the UK, the US and Europe, and for master's and executive programmes, are overseen by our academic director, William Knox. The rest of the people are on the team page.

Questions and answers

My child is in the final year and we are only now thinking about applications. Is it too late?+

No, but fewer levers remain: subjects are chosen and predicted grades set. The statement, tests, interview and shortlist can still be worked on, and timing depends on the course. The consultation shows how much time is really left and which stages are worth buying.

Why pay for a programme when UCAS can be filled in at home?+

It can, and many families do. A programme earns its fee where a mistake is expensive: courses that must share one statement, competitive subjects, Oxbridge, medicine and law. We read the application as an admissions tutor would and find weak points before submission.

Can I buy a single stage?+

Yes, and it is the usual route for families who join late. The starting point is still the consultation and written report, because advice has to rest on the student's current profile. If the consultation has already taken place with ALBION in the same cycle, it is not repeated.

We are not considering Oxford or Cambridge. Is the programme still relevant?+

Yes. The £6,000 programme is built for UK applications generally. Oxbridge has its own track because the deadlines, colleges, tests and interviews are different.

Does my course require an admissions test?+

It depends on the course: LNAT is tied to law, UCAT to medicine, MAT to mathematics at Oxford. The list changes from year to year and should be checked against the university's own site. The page on admissions tests goes through them one by one.

Will ALBION write the personal statement for my child?+

No. A consultant and a subject mentor review drafts and help with positioning, but the voice and the facts belong to the applicant. The page on the personal statement explains how the work runs.
