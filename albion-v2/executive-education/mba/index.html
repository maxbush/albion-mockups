MBA Admissions Consulting: Format, Selection and Strategy

Applicants arrive with different reasons for wanting an MBA: a change of industry, access to an alumni network, a way of settling into a new country. ALBION prepares applications to business schools in Britain and further afield. The first question is what the degree has to do for you, and the choice of school follows from the answer.

What the committee reads

Competition at business schools stays consistently high, and on a number of programmes the selection rate is below 10%. The formal criteria are public and clear. The harder part is what the committee reads between them.

Schools look at more than achievements. They notice how a candidate thinks professionally, the context in which the experience was gained and what the person would add to the room. These signals are easy to miss, especially for applicants from other professional cultures and education systems.

Adult candidates run into a few familiar problems. A career spanning two fields looks like a lack of direction until it is drawn into one story. An essay describes the company when the committee wants to know how the individual will grow. References written for a visa or an employer list projects at length, whereas a school wants an academic reference, which is a different genre. And a long record of achievements can turn into a list of projects in which the main point disappears.

Full-time, accelerated or executive

The formats differ in what happens to your job while you study and in who sits next to you in class. A school's reputation says little about either.

A full-time MBA means a pause in your career. It suits anyone switching sector, function or country, and it is the format in which the alumni community and the school's academic model count for most.

An accelerated MBA compresses the course into a shorter period. It is meant for candidates with substantial experience who cannot justify a long break. The course covers its content in less time, so each week carries more.

An Executive MBA is built around the working manager. Study runs alongside your post, and the cohort is made up of people who already lead. If you are looking for a part-time MBA at senior level, this is probably the format you have in mind. The Oxford version has its own page: Oxford Executive MBA.

An MBA makes sense at a professional turning point, if you hold a management role or are preparing for one, want an international alumni community or see study as part of settling in somewhere new. If none of that applies, we will say so in the first conversation instead of assembling a list for its own sake.

Whichever format you choose, look at the cohort. A director with a wide remit gains little from a class of first-time managers, however well known the school. The length of the course makes no difference to the analysis needed beforehand.

How we assess fit

Anfisa Bashkirova, co-founder of ALBION, took her master's in major programme management at Saïd Business School and takes part in its candidate recruitment as an alumni representative. Out of that experience she built a method for assessing compatibility. Your managerial record is split into strengths and areas to reinforce, then set against the academic model of a given programme.

The method answers one question first: which course fits the direction you want to take. Documents come after that. It grew out of work with one of the more selective business schools, and it applies to other academic models as well. A closer look at the school itself is on the Oxford Saïd MBA page.

What the application contains

The mix depends on the programme. A typical file includes:

  1. Profile analysis and a list of programmes.
  2. Positioning: the story the application tells and how it differs from the others.
  3. CV, motivation statements and essays.
  4. References: the choice of referees and an agreed outline of each letter.
  5. A test, if the programme asks for one. The GMAT or GRE may be a separate stage, prepared with university-level mathematics and English teachers.
  6. Submission, deadline control and interview preparation.

Our team prepares every document, and you approve each one. How the team is organised is described under executive education. If you are comparing several kinds of programme, the page on management programmes explains how masters, diplomas and short courses differ from the MBA.

Questions and answers

What is the difference between an Executive MBA and a full-time MBA?+

An Executive MBA is for people who already hold a management post: the cohort consists of fellow managers, and study runs alongside work. A full-time MBA is built around a career break, so the alumni community and the academic model of the school deserve a closer look.

Is a GMAT or GRE compulsory?+

It depends on the school. Some require a test, some accept an alternative, some do not ask for one. We check the rules once your profile has been reviewed and, if preparation is needed, build it into the plan.

Is there a part time MBA at Oxford?+

The Oxford route for people who keep working is the Executive MBA, taught in modules, and it is described on its own page. The full-time Saïd MBA is a different format. Elsewhere, "part-time" is a label that schools apply to quite different timetables, so look at how the teaching is arranged and who sits in the cohort, not at the name.

How much does an Executive MBA cost?+

Executive MBA cost differs between schools and programmes and is revised with each intake, so we quote no figure here. The school publishes its own tuition, and its website is the place for a current number. We check it against your shortlist when work begins.

Is there a best Executive MBA in the world?+

Rankings are a starting point at most. None of them can say which cohort suits your role and your aims, and that is the question we work on.

Can I apply without a C1 level of English?+

Language requirements differ by programme. Some programmes will consider a waiver if the case for it is well argued, though not every school does. The result depends on the school.

Do you work only with Oxford?+

No, we look at programmes beyond Oxford too. The city is a special case because ALBION is based here and knows the Saïd environment from the inside.

Is an MBA sensible if I own a business?+

Sometimes. A business owner needs to know in advance what the programme would give: a growth strategy, entry to new markets, a network or a change of role. If the answer is not yet clear, that is fine, and we can decide together whether a programme is needed now and which one.
