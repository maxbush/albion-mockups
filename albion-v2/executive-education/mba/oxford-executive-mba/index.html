MBA Admissions Consulting: Format, Selection and Strategy

Oxford Executive MBA: Applying While You Keep Your Post

The Oxford EMBA is taught in modules and built for people who intend to stay in their jobs. That changes what a committee looks for. It is reading for the scale of your responsibility and for the kind of conversation you would bring to a cohort of experienced managers, not for the promise of a junior one. ALBION prepares the documents around your diary: you review drafts, answer questions and approve what is sent.

Choosing between formats

A senior candidate often has a choice of formats, and the choice shapes the application. An executive diploma, a full-time MBA and the Executive MBA each have their own selection logic, so each is a separate application with its own materials. The formats are set side by side on the MBA admissions page.

Where senior applications go wrong

The risks differ from those of a candidate with five years behind them. A profile can be too strong for a shorter programme, and the committee may politely suggest the Executive MBA instead. The remedy is to anchor the application in concrete business tasks: a wish for the qualification itself persuades no school. A long career can produce too many achievements, and a file that lists everything sinks. Most of the editing is cutting.

References are the third trap: the school needs a letter that explains why this person will be a strong member of the group. The fourth is time. A senior person has little of it, so the calendar is planned to limit your part to approvals and a few short working sessions.

Which format suits whom

The decision turns on the time you can give. Not every long programme fits a working diary, and the formats differ in selection logic as well as length.

FormatSuitsNote
Executive MBAA leader who stays in postModular teaching; cohort of experienced managers
Full-time MBASomeone who can step out of work to change field or countryA more classical model, as at the Saïd MBA
Executive DiplomaA candidate who wants a shorter programmeDifferent selection logic; a separate application

We compare the options at the analysis stage so that your effort does not go into a programme that does not solve the problem. Our page on management programmes describes all four groups. If you do not yet hold a leadership post, a classic MBA may fit better, and the MBA admissions overview compares the routes.

How the application is prepared

Clients on this track include senior executives, owners who are scaling or restructuring a business, entrepreneurs entering new markets, and people who moved to the UK for work. The first step is the same for all of them: a review of role, scale of responsibility, career trajectory and the task the degree is meant to serve. We use the compatibility method developed by Anfisa Bashkirova, a Saïd graduate, to judge whether the cohort's level matches yours and whether this is the right moment to apply.

Documents follow: CV, essays, motivation statements and references. Our writers and editors work on them in dialogue with you: the material and the voice stay yours, and you approve each one before submission. Mentors with experience in your industry join the work, and where possible we arrange a conversation with graduates whose profile or aim is close to yours. If a GMAT or GRE is required, university-level mathematics and English teachers handle preparation. The process closes with submission, deadline control and interview rehearsal.

An engagement covers no more than three applications, each treated as a separate programme with its own positioning. The Executive MBA and the Oxford Saïd MBA would count as two.

What we do not promise

A committee makes the decision, so a place cannot be promised. Entry requirements, dates, length of the programme and tuition are set by the school and revised by intake. We check them against the school's pages when work begins. Our fee grows with the number of applications and the amount of preparation, and we give it after an initial conversation.

Questions and answers

Can I do an Executive MBA and keep working?+

That is what the format is designed for: it is modular and does not ask you to leave your post. The load is still considerable, so we discuss early how to spread it around your timetable and travel.

Is a GMAT needed for the Executive MBA?+

It depends on the school and the intake. Some programmes treat a test as a possible stage, others replace it or do not ask for it. We establish this at the start and, if required, build in preparation.

How much does the Oxford Executive MBA cost?+

Tuition is revised by intake, so there is no figure on this page. We verify the current amount at the outset.

What is the Oxford University EMBA, and who runs it?+

It is the Executive MBA of the University of Oxford, run by Saïd Business School. It is also written as the Oxford Said Executive MBA. It is a different programme from the full-time MBA at the same school, with its own cohort and its own application.

Can I apply to both the Executive MBA and the Saïd MBA?+

An engagement with ALBION covers up to three applications, each with its own essays and positioning. Whether the school accepts parallel applications is a question for the rules of the intake. The two also suit different situations, so the question is best settled during analysis.

How much experience do I need?+

The school sets length of service and seniority, and we do not quote a figure without checking the current programme page. In a consultation we judge how closely your profile matches the group, and say so if another format looks better.

I have not written academic texts for a long time. Will I manage?+

Many people start from there. British schools read a personal essay differently from many other systems, and our writers and editors help to put the text together. The final version remains yours.
