Oxford Acceptance Rate: What the Statistics Show

For 2024 entry, 23,061 people applied to Oxford, 3,793 received an offer and 3,245 were admitted. Cambridge, in the same cycle, received 22,153 applications, made 4,760 offers and accepted 3,632 students. The Oxford acceptance rate is usually quoted as a single figure, but it can be calculated in several ways, and none of them predicts the outcome for a particular applicant.

The numbers for both universities

This is the latest cycle for which we have checked the official data of both universities. New figures appear once a year.

ApplicationsOffersAdmittedOffers per applicationAdmitted per application
Oxford23,0613,7933,24516.4%14.1%
Cambridge22,1534,7603,63221.5%16.4%

The percentages are our own arithmetic on the universities' published totals. Cambridge also publishes an overall success rate of 16.4%, which matches the share of applications that ended in an accepted place.

Three ways to count the rate

An offer rate counts applications that ended in a place being offered, including conditional offers. Some candidates miss their grades or decline, so fewer are admitted than receive offers: 3,245 against 3,793 at Oxford. The share admitted out of all applications is the strictest measure, and it is the one most people mean by "acceptance rate".

Compare like with like: offers with offers, admissions with admissions. The overall figures of the two universities are close, yet the spread between courses inside each of them is wider than the gap between the two.

The distance between Computer Science and the languages is more than sevenfold. In Mathematics there were 537 offers but only 260 acceptances, so a large share of offers never turns into a place. Oxford publishes comparable course data in its annual admissions report.

Overseas applicants face different odds

Oxford breaks applicants down by domicile. For 2024:

DomicileApplicationsOffersAdmittedOffers per application
United Kingdom14,6942,9962,61220.4%
EU1,4921251038.4%
Rest of the world6,8756725309.8%

We worked out the offers and admissions for the rest of the world by subtracting the UK and EU figures from the totals. UK applicants received an offer in about one case in five, and applicants from outside the EU in about one in ten. Applicants from outside the EU made up 29.8% of the pool and 16.3% of those admitted.

The grades of those who are admitted

Oxford states that its minimum standard offer for A-Level candidates is three A grades, and that many courses, particularly in the sciences, require at least one A*. A*AA or better was achieved by 54.1% of applicants and 85.6% of admitted students, and 45.5% of those admitted had three A* grades or better. Grades are necessary but not sufficient: more than half of applicants already held A*AA, and roughly one in seven was admitted.

What the rate does not tell you

An overall figure averages courses, countries and profiles. It leaves out admissions tests, interviews and the way an application is put together, which are exactly what separates candidates with the same grades. Judging your own chances from the headline rate is therefore unreliable. The better guide is the course, the university's test requirements and the student's real level.

Three in four of ALBION's candidates are invited to an Oxbridge interview, and 100% of our clients receive an offer from at least one Russell Group university. Those are the results of work on individual profiles, not a promise to the next family, and they do not replace the general statistics. Our approach to course, test and application is set out on the page about Oxford and Cambridge admission.

What to do with these numbers

  1. Check the chosen course against the table and weigh its competition against the student's profile.
  2. Find out whether an admissions test is required and put it in the plan. The page on university admissions tests explains which exist.
  3. Prepare the interview in the subject early. The interview guide explains how it works.
  4. Mind the date: applications to Oxford, Cambridge and most medical courses are normally due by 15 October, and UCAS allows only one of the two universities.

Questions and answers

What is the Oxford acceptance rate?+

For 2024 entry, 16.4% of applications received an offer and 14.1% ended in admission. The figure is an overall one: it differs noticeably by course and by country, and it changes every year.

Is it harder to get into Oxford than into Cambridge?+

On the overall figures for 2024, Oxford admitted a smaller share of applications: 14.1% against 16.4%. The spread between courses inside each university is wider than that gap, so compare specific courses.

Are the odds lower for an overseas applicant?+

According to Oxford's 2024 data, about 20% of UK applicants received an offer, against about 10% of applicants from outside the EU. These shares describe competition overall and do not predict the result of an individual application.

The figures come from the official admissions statistics of Oxford and Cambridge for 2024 and were checked on 15 October 2026.
