The 11+ Exam: Grammar Schools and Independent Schools Are Not the Same Thing

The 11+ exam is a name for two different processes. One leads into grammar schools, which are state schools that select by test. The other is a set of entrance assessments set by independent schools for Year 7. The tests, dates and rules do not match, so preparation starts by finding out which school is meant.

Book a consultation

The 11+ for grammar schools

Grammar schools are state schools. The government describes them as schools that can be run by the local authority, a foundation body or an academy trust, that select their pupils on academic ability, and that set a test for entry. Free state education is for children who are entitled to it. For families without status in the UK these schools are generally closed, so that question is settled before any preparation begins.

The 11+ for independent schools

Entry at 11 is to Year 7, and each school sets its own combination of assessments. The usual ones are the ISEB Pre-Test, in some schools CAT4 or CEM Select, the school's own papers, Common Entrance 11+ and an interview.

The ISEB Pre-Test is an adaptive online test: its difficulty adjusts to the child's answers, and age is allowed for in scoring. It covers English, maths, and verbal and non-verbal reasoning. A parent or guardian registers the child through the ISEB portal, and each school decides its own scores and dates. The Pre-Test is taken in Year 6 or 7, and the school uses it to decide whether to invite the child further. For children from overseas some schools accept UKiset, which compares the candidate with British peers and whose result can be sent to several schools in the network. The tests are covered in detail on our page about entrance exams for private schools and in the guide to entrance tests.

The fees are a working figure, and the school gives the exact amount. Day schools and how they work are covered on our page about day schools.

How to prepare

There is no single set of tests, so the first step is to draw up a list of schools and find out what each one requires. For a child who is not at a British school, English is checked first: if the child cannot yet follow the tasks comfortably in English, the result will understate their real level. An academic assessment checks English first as well.

The Pre-Test and CAT4 cannot be learned like a syllabus, but a child can get used to the format. ISEB publishes materials for families openly, so a family can practise without a tutor. A tutor is needed where mock results show a specific weakness: reading the question, speed, logic sequences.

Three common mistakes

  1. Treating the 11+ as a single exam when schools set different ones.
  2. Preparing for a test that is not in the set used by the shortlisted schools.
  3. Putting off the English check for a child who is not at a British school.

Questions and answers

What is the 11+ exam?+

It is the general name for selection at the age of 11, for entry to Year 7. For grammar schools it is a test for a place at a state school, and for independent schools it is a set of assessments that each school chooses.

Can an overseas child go to a grammar school?+

Generally not, if the family has no UK status, because state schools admit under their own rules. Status is checked before preparation starts, and the choice then narrows to independent schools.

When is the 11+ taken?+

The Pre-Test falls in Year 6 or 7. Each school decides its own dates and pass marks, so the timing is checked school by school, and preparation begins early.

Does a child need a tutor for the 11+?+

Not always. ISEB publishes materials for families, and part of the preparation can be done independently. A tutor is needed when mock papers show a particular weakness.
