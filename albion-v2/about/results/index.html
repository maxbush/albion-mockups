About ALBION

Results and figures

96% of ALBION pupils are placed at the school they chose. That figure and the others describe past years. None is a forecast or a promise to the next family, because a place or an offer comes from the school or university, not from the consultant.

Book a consultation

How much weight the numbers can bear

The figures are the company's own, without an outside audit. Only the Saïd figure carries a time frame. Treat the rest as a guide, not as admissions statistics.

University offers and Oxbridge interviews

An offer from at least one Russell Group university has gone to 100% of clients. That need not be the first course or university on the list, and an offer may be conditional on final grades.

Oxbridge is counted separately: 3 in 4 of the students ALBION works with are invited to interview. An invitation is a stage in selection and not an offer, and the decision then rests with the college admissions process. The figure does not say how many of those invited went on to receive an offer.

Schools

The 96% does not mean every child received a first choice without complication. The stories listed below include a waiting list and an outcome that differed from the original aim. Nor does it promise anything to the next child, since nobody can predict how an application will fare in a given year.

Adult clients

Over the last year, 10+ adult clients have completed programmes at Saïd Business School, University of Oxford. These are graduates, not the number admitted this year. Admission is the school's decision. The method Anfisa Bashkirova designed for testing how a professional profile fits a programme helps choose the right one and has no bearing on the committee.

Tutoring

ALBION has 150+ tutors across time zones, and together they teach 3,000+ hours a year. Both numbers describe capacity: how many people and hours stand behind the preparation. Neither measures how an individual pupil will do. Nor will a child be taught by just any of the 150. The tutor is matched to the task, and Jonathan Mintram chooses each one personally.

Years in practice and client ratings

ALBION has worked in British education since 2010. That tells you the firm has seen many admissions cycles, not that its approach will suit your child. Its rating on Google is 5.0. Clients write those reviews themselves, and the reviews page explains how to read them.

Where the family stories are

The anonymised stories appear on the pages whose subjects they illustrate.

  • Private schools: an applicant at 18+ holds two offers, then one school withdraws its place.
  • Boarding schools: a girl applying for Year 9 holds two offers while her first choice puts her on a waiting list.
  • Entrance exams: two years at a prep school before Year 9 entry, then offers from other selective schools.
  • Tutoring: a Year 10 entry that did not happen, and a Year 9 place that did.
  • GCSE tutors: a one-year retake instead of a direct route to A-Level.
  • A-Level tutors: a pupil aged 18 or over and the adjustments for her exams.

Questions and answers

Can I trust these figures?+

They cannot be fully checked from outside, because they are the firm's own data. For that reason the percentages should not be read as admissions statistics. Rely more on what is visible: fees are published, the team is named, and clients' ratings can be read on Google.

What does 96% mean, and what happens to the other 4%?+

The figure does not say. When an application fails to produce an offer, the options are another school on the list, another entry point or a longer period of preparation. That is why a list is never built around a single name.

Why do the stories carry no school names or family names?+

They are published anonymised. The drawback is plain: a particular case cannot be checked by name, and we do not hide that.

Where are the client reviews?+

There are no quotations on this page. Clients leave reviews on Google, and the reviews page explains how to read them.

Does 100% for Russell Group mean my child will receive an offer?+

No. The figure describes clients of past years. Your child's result will depend on subjects, grades, the application and the university's decision.
