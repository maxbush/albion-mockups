# MBA in the UK: How to Choose and How to Get In

A one-year British MBA is a concentrated investment: the Oxford and Cambridge programmes run twelve months against two years in the US, at roughly half the opportunity cost. But the format suits a specific career question — and the wrong answer costs a year and the fee.

## The schools that matter

- **Oxford Saïd** — one year, strong in entrepreneurship and social impact; the collegiate university environment is part of the product.
- **Cambridge Judge** — one year, smaller cohorts, strong ties to the tech cluster.
- **London Business School** — flexible 15–21 months, the strongest finance pipeline in Europe.
- **Imperial College Business School** — technology and innovation focus.
- **Warwick, Manchester (Alliance)** — strong mid-tier options with softer competition.

## What the application requires

- **GMAT/GRE** — a competitive score for the top schools sits around 680+ (GMAT Focus equivalents vary); some profiles qualify for waivers.
- **Essays** — each school asks its own questions; the career goal must be concrete and the school's role in it named.
- **CV** — one page, trajectory over chronology.
- **Two professional references.**
- **Interview** — invitation-only; at Saïd, conducted largely by alumni.
- **IELTS/TOEFL** — unless prior education was in English.

## Rounds and timing

British MBAs admit in several rounds from September onward. Earlier rounds are statistically kinder — more places, more scholarships. A realistic runway for a strong package is 3–6 months before the round you target.

## Costs and funding

- **Tuition:** Oxford/Cambridge/LBS — ⏳ ~£85,000–115,000 (verify current figures); living costs in Oxford ~£1,800–2,200/month.
- **Scholarships** — merit awards at all schools; some are automatic with the application, others need a separate essay. For international candidates this is the main lever on cost.

## Questions

**MBA at 30+ — too late?** No; average age at Oxford is ~29. The question is what the year is for, not the birthday.
**Do I need the GMAT really?** For the top schools — almost always yes, unless a waiver applies. We test the waiver route first.
**Scholarships for international students?** Yes — partial awards are common at top schools; full scholarships are rare and hard-fought.
