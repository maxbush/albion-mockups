# University Admissions in the UK

## The route, not the form

A British university place is not "apply in September". It is a route that begins two to four years out: the choice of A-Level or IB subjects determines which faculties open at all; predicted grades set the corridor of applications; admissions tests and interviews measure what school grades cannot show.

ALBION is an Oxford-based consultancy, working since 2010. We manage admission end to end — from subject choices and the university shortlist to tutoring, the UCAS application and decisions on offers. Our office is in Oxford: we see this system from inside, not from brochures.

## Honest about tiers: where admission is realistic

Britain has roughly 160 universities, and "good" depends on the subject, not only the name. We look at three layers:

- **Oxbridge** — a separate deadline (15 October), its own tests and interviews. Exceptional competition; we prepare those with an objective chance and say so honestly to those without one.
- **Russell Group and strong specialist universities** — LSE, Imperial, UCL, Durham, leading programmes beyond the famous twenty. Here predicted grades, the statement and the profile decide — not grades alone.
- **The right university for the goal** — a strong department matters more than overall ranking: in a number of subjects the best choice sits outside the famous names.

The typical mistake is filing five applications "by ranking". We build the list on the mathematics of competition: ambitious, working and reserve choices — the same method as for schools.

## The mechanics

**UCAS.** One system, one form, five choices, one personal statement. The deadline for Oxbridge, medicine, veterinary and dentistry is 15 October; for the rest, late January. Errors in the form, the dates or the choice of five are the most common cause of a lost year.

**Personal statement.** Universities read not motivation in the essay sense but evidence of academic interest: what has been read, researched and done to support the chosen subject.

**Admissions tests.** Mathematics and engineering — the MAT/PAT/ESAT family; law — LNAT; some universities run their own. Formats change; we prepare to current specifications, not last year's descriptions.

**Interviews.** At Oxbridge the decisive stage: an academic conversation testing thinking, not memorised answers. We prepare with subject tutors, including those who have taught within the Oxford system.

## Oxbridge — separately and without promises

A frequent request is "Oxford at any cost". Our position is honest: offers are not for sale. What decides is predicted grades (typically A*AA and above), tests, interview and profile — what the candidate has read, researched and lived with the subject.

> **In practice** — anonymised. A pupil with a strong profile aimed at Oxbridge. He reached the interview — the most selective stage — but did not receive an offer. We rebuilt the list and closed the year with a strong Russell Group university, where he is now studying successfully. Reaching the interview means the preparation was right; the outcome of the interview is a competition no one can guarantee.

The adult track is more predictable: over the past year 10+ of our clients completed programmes at Saïd Business School (Oxford) — executive admissions run on different rules, where experience counts for more than exams.

## Master's degrees and executive education

A separate track for adults: master's degrees at British universities including Oxford, MBA and executive programmes. Here the work is with the motivation profile, references and the career logic of the application — the mechanics differ from undergraduate admissions.

## Questions

**What grades does Oxford require?**
A typical conditional offer is A*AA or above at A-Level (or an IB equivalent of 40+), plus admissions tests and interview. But grades are only the threshold: selection is decided by tests and interview, not the average mark.

**At what age do students enter?**
Standard entry follows A-Level at 18. Underage students require a guardian in the UK — that contour is built into our work.

**Is there funding for international students?**
Full funding at undergraduate level is rare; partial scholarships exist, and stronger schemes at master's level. We calculate the realistic options for your profile at consultation.

**How do you differ from an agency that files documents?**
We do not fill in forms — we build the route: subjects, tests, interviews, profile. Filing the documents is the last and simplest step.
