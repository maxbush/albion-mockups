# Private Day Schools in England

## The honest fork first

A day school is the format for a family that lives in the UK or is prepared to relocate. That is the first question we ask — not "which school is best" but "where will the family live".

- **The family lives in or is moving to the UK** → day school: the child is home every evening, the choice runs from central London to the counties.
- **The family stays abroad** → [boarding school + guardianship → /private-schools/boarding/]: a day place is impossible without a parent on the ground.

Sales-driven agencies sometimes offer day schools "with accommodation arrangements" — in practice that is either a boarding contract in different packaging or a grey scheme. We run both scenarios and tell you plainly which one fits.

## Types of day school

- **Pre-prep (ages 4–7)** — the junior stage; entry is gentle but London demand is high, and registration often happens at birth.
- **Prep school (7–13)** — the preparatory stage: main entries at 7+/8+, a direct route into strong senior schools at 11+/13+.
- **Senior day schools (11/13–18)** — from academically selective London schools (the toughest competition in the country) to solid county schools.
- **Grammar schools** — free selective state schools: open only to residents, and only those living in the catchment. If you are not resident, this layer is simply unavailable — and we say so at once.

## Geography decides more than rankings

In the day format, the address is daily logistics: 30–45 minutes to school is normal, an hour or more is a daily load on the child. London's selective schools cluster in a handful of districts; county schools draw from wider territory with softer competition. We select the school around the family's route — not the family's life around a famous postcode.

## Entry points — tougher competition than boarding

London day schools are the most competitive entry point in the British system: 7+/8+ and 11+ select harder here than many boarding schools do at 13+. Exams are set by the school or run through shared systems, plus interviews and assessment days.

> **In practice** — anonymised. A family planned 13+ entry to a leading boarding school. We advised instead moving the boy to a strong prep for Years 7–8 — two years of settling and preparation. The first round of top-school applications produced no offers; we rebuilt the list around his profile — and he received places at every school in the second round, including applications filed after deadlines.

## Costs — counted honestly

- **Day school:** ~£6,000–9,000 per term (London sits at the upper bound and above).
- **Plus the cost of living near the school:** renting in the right London district can easily double the budget — the annual total often matches boarding, where accommodation is already included.
- **Guardianship:** an international pupil under 18 still needs a guardian at a day school — for out-of-hours and emergencies. [How guardianship works →](/private-schools/guardianship/)

## Questions

**Can a child attend a day school while the family lives abroad?**
No — the day format requires a parent nearby every day. If the family is not relocating, boarding is the workable format.

**What about weekly or flexi-boarding?**
A middle path many schools offer: the child boards Monday to Friday and is home at weekends. It suits families within reasonable reach who want immersion without full boarding — we check which schools on a shortlist run the scheme, as availability varies widely.

**Day or boarding — which is "better"?**
Neither is better; one will fit. Day gives daily family contact; boarding gives immersion and independence. Given the child's temperament and the family's logistics, the answer is usually obvious after assessment.

**When should we register?**
London pre-preps — from birth; prep schools — one to two years before entry. Late windows do occur, but the choice narrows.
