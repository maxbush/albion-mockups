# Cases

Real work, anonymised. We do not publish names, schools or identifying details — the point of a case is the decision, not the family. Where a school name is shown in brackets, it awaits permission.

## School admissions

**Four sixth-form offers.**
A pupil received offers from [SCHOOLS: Magdalen College School, Highgate, National Mathematics and Science College] and chose [SCHOOL: d'Overbroeck's]. The list was built for fit, not rankings — and it worked at every entry. ⏳

**A place lost in July, recovered by September.**
A pupil aged 18+ held a boarding place, visa in progress, when the school withdrew it after an inspection. We rebuilt the application and closed the year with another strong school. One offer is not a plan. ⏳ details with Anfisa

**A waiting-list win, ten days before term.**
A Year 9 pupil held two offers while her first-choice school kept her waiting. We managed the list actively — a place opened ten days before term, and because the decision had been made in advance, the family moved. ⏳

**A full GCSE programme from scratch.**
A home-educated pupil — a tournament chess player, English below school level, suspected dyslexia — completed the whole GCSE programme with our tutors and won an offer from a strong school for A-Level at 14. ⏳

## University admissions

**Interview at Oxbridge — and a strong plan B.**
A candidate reached the Oxbridge interview stage — no offer came. We rebuilt the list and closed the year with a strong Russell Group university. We show this honestly: reaching interview is a preparation result; the interview itself is a competition. ⏳

**Fifteen applications over the holidays.**
A family arrived during the Christmas break; within two weeks about fifteen applications were filed. An extreme timeline — but it shows what a controlled process can compress. ⏳

**Five offers, one turn in August.**
Offers from all five universities on the list — then plans changed in August. The strategy was rebuilt after results; the strong option was preserved. ⏳

## Guardianship

**Ninety conduct marks — and one missing question.**
A pupil was accumulating disciplinary notes; the school recorded symptoms. We asked why, arranged specialist assessment, and the school received a support plan instead of sanctions. ⏳ guardianship lead review

**The fracture the nurse did not see.**
A child's pain was dismissed by the school. We insisted on an independent examination — a fracture. A guardian stands on the child's side when the system is in a hurry. ⏳

**A school place under threat — turned around.**
A first term of academic and behavioural problems put a school place in doubt. Assessment, a specialist plan, coordination with the school. A year on: steady progress, and the family is looking at stronger sixth forms. ⏳

## Executive education

**10+ clients to Saïd in one year.**

**A pivot, not a continuation.**
A client asked for "an MBA"; diagnostics showed the real goal was an industry change. A management master's was the better instrument — shorter, cheaper, correctly read by employers. ⏳
