# Career guidance for school students

Few choices in a British education look like career decisions when they are made. Picking subjects at 13 or 14, A-Levels or the IB at 16, a degree course at 17: each narrows the next. ALBION's career guidance puts the whole sequence on one page, so that a student chooses subjects with the destination in mind and a family sees what a course leads to before applying to it.

## Where the decisions fall

| Stage | What it commonly shapes |
|---|---|
| GCSE options | which sixth-form subjects are open |
| A-Level or IB choices | which degree courses the student can apply for |
| University course | the profession, or the postgraduate route, that follows |

A weak combination tends to show itself late, when little can be done before the application deadline. Advice on subject choice is therefore written with the later application in mind, Oxford and Cambridge included, where depth in the subject carries particular weight.

## How we look at a degree

A university is a tool, not an end in itself. A well-produced prospectus and a lively student scene are poor reasons to pick a course. We assess each university and each programme by asking who the student will be ten years on, and not just on graduation three or four years from now.

## What the work involves

Guidance starts from the student's present profile: subjects, results and interests. Where a clearer view of academic level is needed, an [academic assessment](/assessment/) is added. We then map how the chosen subjects connect with courses, and what each combination would leave closed.

Beyond lessons, an adviser discusses what could strengthen a future application: summer schools, lectures and masterclasses, essay competitions, olympiads, internships and work experience in the relevant field where such options exist. We also go through what the student has read and done, to see which of it reflects real interest in the subject. Once the direction is clear, the next step is usually [university admissions](/university-admissions/) or [master's admissions](/university-admissions/masters/), with [subject tutoring](/tutors/) for exams on the way and [summer schools](/summer-schools/) for the holidays.

## What it does not do

We will not name a single correct career, and a plan made at 14 will be revised as interests change. The number of sessions and the fee depend on the student's age, the aim and the amount of work. Both are discussed at an initial consultation, to which we invite you after the enquiry form.

## Questions and answers

### When should a student start?

Earlier gives more room. The most useful moments are just before GCSE options and again before sixth-form subjects, since both define which programmes stay open.

### Is career guidance the same as an academic assessment?

No. An assessment shows a student's level against peers and where the gaps lie. Guidance is about direction: which subjects and courses lead where. The two can be combined when a family needs both.

### What happens afterwards?

The family chooses a route: applications to universities in the UK, the US or Europe, a master's, or continued school work with tutors. The decision stays with the family, and the preparation plan follows the route chosen.
