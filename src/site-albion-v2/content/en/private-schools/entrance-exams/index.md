# Entrance exams for UK independent schools: 7+ to 16+

Parents tend to ask which exam their child has to sit, the 11+ exam or the 13+. The better question is which schools, because the UK has no single set of school entrance exams: each school asks for its own combination of results, depending on the entry point (7+, 8+, 11+, 13+ or 16+), the shortlist and where the child studies now. At ALBION the order is fixed: schools first, then the tests those schools use, then the lessons.

## Which entrance exam at which age

Schools change their requirements from year to year, so the table is a general map. We confirm the exact combination school by school.

| Entry point | Year group | What candidates typically sit | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7+ and 8+ | Years 3 and 4, prep school | Prep school entrance exams, set by each school; there is no common package | Only the school can say what a seven- or eight-year-old will be asked to do at its 7+ or 8+ exam |
| 11+ | Year 7 | ISEB Pre-Test; in some schools CAT4 or CEM Select; the school's own papers; Common Entrance at 11+; interview | The Pre-Test is taken in Year 6 or 7, and the school uses it to decide whom to invite further |
| 13+ | Year 9 | In some schools the Pre-Test as a first filter; then Common Entrance at 13+; CASE for scholarships; interview | The Pre-Test falls in Year 6 or 7, well before Common Entrance |
| 16+ | Year 12, sixth form | The school's own papers in the intended A-Level subjects; interview | There is no common sixth form entrance exam: students choose their subjects, and the school tests readiness for those |
| Any entry point, applying from overseas | By school | UKiset, where the school accepts it | The result compares the child with British peers and can go to several schools |

The first row leads to prep school. The others are senior school entrance exams, for [boarding](/private-schools/boarding/) or [day](/private-schools/day-schools/) places.

## Where your child is starting from

A child at a British prep school usually follows the route in the table. One at a local school abroad may need UKiset or a school's online tasks, with English checked first. For a child at an international school on the British curriculum we compare the level with British independent schools before choosing tests.

### What is UKiset, and who takes it

UKiset is an adaptive test for international students applying to British schools, developed with Cambridge University Press & Assessment. Most candidates sit the UKiset test online under remote invigilation. It measures academic potential and English against British pupils of the same age. Schools tend to read the UKiset score as a profile before the rest of the file, not as a pass or fail mark.

One set of UKiset results can go to several schools in the UKiset network. Schools that use UKiset are a growing group, not all of them, so we check your list before anyone registers.

## What the other tests involve

### ISEB Pre-Test

The Independent Schools Examinations Board (ISEB) runs this test, also called the Common Pre-Test, online. It is adaptive, so difficulty follows the child's answers, and the score is standardised for age. It covers English, maths, and verbal and non-verbal reasoning. A parent registers the child through the ISEB portal; each senior school sets its own thresholds and timetable.

ISEB publishes preparation material for families, so ISEB Pre-Test preparation can start at home. A tutor helps where practice shows a specific weakness: pace, pattern sequences, reading the question.

### CAT4, CEM Select and Quest

The CAT4 test measures verbal, non-verbal, quantitative and spatial reasoning. Schools use it at 11+ and 13+, and after entry to place children in sets. The CEM Select and Quest admissions tests are requested by individual schools, so the school confirms which applies. None of these can be learned like a syllabus, but the format can be met in advance.

### Common Entrance and CASE

The Common Entrance exam, set by ISEB, is sat at 11+ or 13+ in subjects the school specifies, so Common Entrance preparation follows the school's list. CASE is the 13+ scholarship route. A child from an international school may be offered the school's own papers instead, so it is worth asking before lessons are booked.

### Interview

Many schools interview, from prep school to sixth form. Closer to the dates ALBION runs mock interviews on subjects and on the child, with feedback, so the real one is not new.

## How 11+, 13+ and 16+ exam preparation is organised

The person choosing the schools and the person teaching your child sit in the same team. If a mock paper shows that a shortlisted school is out of reach for now, the shortlist and the lesson plan are revised in one conversation. ALBION does not act for individual schools, so the list is built around the child.

The sequence below is the one described for 16+ sixth form entry. At 11+ and 13+ the mix of tests and lessons depends on the schools on the list.

1. [Academic assessment](/assessment/): online, with ALBION teachers, on past papers, set against British peers.
2. A shortlist. For sixth form it starts at up to ten schools, with five recommended for applications.
3. Lessons shaped by the formats of the chosen schools: closing gaps, exam practice, online tests. Intensity follows the assessment; subjects and tutors can change.
4. Mock exams in the school's format, then mock interviews.
5. Feedback on lessons and readiness every quarter.

## When the plan has to change

Sometimes about six months remain, or key deadlines have gone. The schools most in demand settle their lists long before entry, and that window does not reopen. What remains is schools with later intakes, stronger English and a different entry point.

One boy, then in Year 6 of a British school, came to us with Year 9 entry to a well-known boarding school in mind. His ability was not in doubt, but his English and schoolwork needed time, so we suggested two further years at a co-educational boarding prep school. The applications to the most selective senior schools, which we backed while being frank about the odds, brought no offers. We then approached other selective schools, some after their usual deadlines. Each made an offer, and he now attends the school his family chose.

## Questions and answers

### How early should we start?

There is no single answer: each school has its own registration window and tests. The Pre-Test is taken in Year 6 or 7, so for 11+ and 13+ the clock starts before the year of entry, and earlier still for the most sought-after schools. We confirm dates with each school when the list is drawn up.

### What happens if my child does not do well in a test?

The school reads the result alongside the rest of the file and may offer a place, a waiting-list position or a refusal. Another school, another entry point or longer preparation remain possible.

### Can a test be retaken?

Retake rules are set by exam boards and schools and vary from test to test, so we ask before registration.

### UKiset vs ISEB Pre-Test: which does my child take?

They are separate tests. The ISEB Pre-Test is set by ISEB and taken in Year 6 or 7; UKiset is the profile for international applicants, accepted by schools in its network. The school decides which it wants.

### Where is UKiset taken, and are there UKiset sample questions?

Most candidates sit it online through the UKiset invigilation service, so UKiset test centres are not usually part of the plan. The test is adaptive, so sample questions illustrate the format and do not forecast what your child will see. A UKiset practice test, taken online, is where UKiset preparation starts. A tutor then works on the English and reasoning it measures.

### My child's English is weak. Is that a barrier?

It is the first thing we measure. The plan then starts with English lessons, and schools and entry point are chosen with the time available in mind.

### Are 11+ lessons online or in person?

Mostly online, as tutors work across time zones. In-person teaching can be arranged for families who want it; we confirm whether a tutor can come to you once subjects and dates are known.
