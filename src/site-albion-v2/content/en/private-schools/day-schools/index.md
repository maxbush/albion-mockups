# Private Day Schools in London and the UK: Prep, 11+ and Senior

Some families look at day schools because they already live in the UK, others because they are about to move. Either way the school has to be within a daily commute. If you will stay overseas, the realistic route is usually a [boarding school](/private-schools/boarding/) with a guardian. ALBION works with both, and we will not recommend a day school to a family that needs boarding.

## Prep school: what it is and how to enter

A prep school is a preparatory school for children from about seven or eight up to 13, and it may include a pre-prep section for ages four to seven. Pre prep schools can also stand alone, with children moving on to a prep school at 7+ or 8+. Entry at 7+ or 8+ is decided by the school's own assessment in English, maths and reasoning, and a short interview. The prep years give a child time to settle into the British system and to build the academic base for 13+ entry to a senior school.

| | Prep school | Senior day school |
|---|---|---|
| Ages | 7 or 8 to 13 (pre-prep 4–7) | 11 or 13 to 18 |
| Entry points | 7+, 8+ | 11+, 13+, 16+ |
| How applicants are assessed | School assessment, interview | ISEB Pre-Test, CAT4, Common Entrance, school papers, interview |
| What to look at | Where leavers go at 13; how the school prepares for 13+ | A-Level or IB, pastoral system, sixth form |

## 11+ in London

11+ is the entry point most London families ask about first. Independent schools in London, UK, set their own requirements: some use the ISEB Pre-Test and CAT4, some add their own papers, and most hold an interview. Registration and testing dates differ by school, so check them early and do not rely on last year's calendar.

Competition for day places at 7+, 8+ and 11+ in London is often higher than for boarding places at the same ages, which makes a shortlist of five matter even more. Preparation for the tests and interviews is delivered by ALBION tutors, and our page on [entrance exams at 11+, 13+ and 16+](/private-schools/entrance-exams/) describes each test.

## Day, boarding or both

The choice is about how your family lives, not about which format is superior.

A family resident in the UK has the widest set of options, and a day school keeps the child at home. A family relocating for the school should usually choose the school first and the house second. A family overseas with a child of 11 or older is usually looking at boarding. If you live in the UK but travel a lot, there is a third model: a day school plus academic and organisational coordination, which ALBION can provide on request. It covers meetings with the school, monitoring of progress, and communication between school and parents.

For an international family without UK status, state and grammar schools are usually closed, so the practical question is which independent day school suits the child.

## What are the best private schools in the UK?

There is no single best school, only the best fit for a particular child, so the best private schools in England for one family may be wrong for another. League tables mainly show how selective a school is on entry, and a list of the top private schools in England tells you who gets in rather than what the school adds. The better criteria are how competitive entry is for your child, the academic level the child can sustain, the pastoral system and the daily commute.

ALBION does not publish rankings or name schools. The list of up to ten, and the five we advise applying to, comes from the child's assessment and the family's address, not from a table.

## What it costs

The published market range for a private day school is £6,000–9,000 per term, over three terms a year. A registration fee, exam fees and travel come on top, and so does the family's cost of living in the city, which is why the yearly total matters more than the fee alone.

ALBION's fee for a day-school admission follows the child's year group, the number of schools on the list and the hours of preparation. We quote it once we have the application form and have spoken with you. Tutoring hours are billed separately at [published rates](/prices/).

## How an application runs

The process mirrors boarding, with distance from home added as a criterion. It starts with an [academic assessment](/assessment/): teachers compare the child with British peers at independent schools. Consultants then draw up a list of up to ten schools and recommend applying to five. After that come preparation, applications and correspondence with schools. A relocating family can ask us to help find housing near the school.

We take no commission from schools, so the list reflects the child and the commute, not a partner network. The full set of stages is described in our overview of [private schools in the UK](/private-schools/).

## From practice

This case involves a boarding school, but its logic is general. The family approached us while their son was in Year 6 of a British school. His ability was clear, but his English and academic preparation needed strengthening. They hoped for Year 9 entry to a highly selective senior boarding school, and ALBION suggested two years first, covering Years 7 and 8, at a strong prep school that taught boys and girls together.

Applications to the most selective schools brought no offers. We went back to the question of what he needed, in lessons and in personal support, and approached other selective schools, some of them after their usual deadlines. Every one offered him a place. The prep school was a boarding one, and ALBION's guardianship team worked with the family through his first year. Giving a child two years before 13+ does not depend on the format, but this case has not been tested with a day school.

## Questions and answers

### Is a day school cheaper than boarding?

The school fee is usually lower. In London, though, housing and daily logistics are added, and the total can end up close to boarding. Compare yearly costs for the whole family.

### Which are the best private schools in London?

That depends on the child and the commute, not on a table, because private schools in London, England, differ widely in selectivity, size and ethos. Competition for day places in London is often high, so a shortlist built around your child's level and your address works better than a list of famous names. We draw it up after the academic assessment.

### Is there a list of the top 10 private schools in the UK?

Many are published, but they mostly rank selectivity and exam results, and a top 10 hides the fit between child and school. ALBION does not publish one. Which of the top private schools in the UK suits a child comes from the assessment, the entry point and where the family lives.

### Can an international child go to a grammar school?

As a rule, no. Grammar schools are state-funded and selective, admitting by an 11+ test, and they are open to families with UK status. For international families the main route is independent schools.

### What is a catchment area?

It is the area around a state school from which pupils are given priority. State schools allocate places mainly by where the family lives, while independent schools select through tests and interviews.

### How do we choose between schools in London and in the counties around it?

Most families choose the school first and then the area, because the daily commute shapes a child's routine more than expected. We draw up the list of schools before looking at neighbourhoods.

### Does my child need a guardian if they attend a day school?

If the parents live in the UK, no. If they live elsewhere, a British school requires an official guardian for a child under 18, and a day school is no exception. Our [guardianship page](/private-schools/guardianship/) explains how ALBION handles it.

### When should we start preparing for 11+ in London?

Each school sets its own registration and test dates, so ask early and do not assume last year's calendar will repeat. The sooner the assessment is done, the more time there is to prepare. On a short timetable the list is built differently.
