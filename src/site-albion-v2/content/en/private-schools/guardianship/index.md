# Educational guardianship for pupils at UK schools

A UK school will normally expect a pupil under 18 who has come from abroad to have a named guardian in the country. This page explains guardianship for international students at UK schools: what the role covers and how ALBION carries it out, to the AEGIS standard. We take on few pupils and do not advertise the service.

## What an educational guardian in the UK does

In school guardianship in the UK the school entrusts the guardian with the child's health and wellbeing and with practical decisions: travel, accommodation, exeat weekends and holidays. For a boarding school guardian, exeat weekends and holidays are where the role is most visible, because the child is away from school. For the parents the guardian is their representative in the UK, acting for the child where they cannot be present.

A guardian does not stand in for the parents. The role is a responsible adult with the authority to act.

## Who we can take on

Our UK guardianship services are limited to a few places. Guardianship, as we run it, is personal and needs a consultant who stays closely involved, so we offer it chiefly to pupils whose admission the ALBION team handled. Where we did not guide the placement, we are unlikely to be able to help.

There is no fee on this page. Terms are agreed with families whose [boarding school admission](/private-schools/boarding/) or other school placement ALBION is managing. Pupils at day schools can have academic and practical coordination if the family asks for it.

## What the work involves

The guardian is an ALBION consultant who has known the pupil since admission: academic and personal profile, goals, how the child has settled. A family therefore does not have to explain the background each time a new question arises.

The consultant attends meetings with the school, reviews academic progress and checks that agreements are kept. The consultant also keeps school and parents informed of each other, so that neither hears of a problem last, and explains the British system, the programmes and the school's internal procedures. Where a pupil is doing steadily well, the aim is to hold that level, adjust the subject load and plan the next steps. Where difficulties appear, whether academic, behavioural or practical, the consultant joins the conversations with the school and draws up a plan to keep the pupil on course.

Specialists are brought in when needed: educational psychologists, study skills coaches, subject [tutors](/tutors/), specialists in assessing learning needs and medical consultants. The guardianship team is led by ALBION co-founder Altynai Yeshmatova, who is introduced on the [team page](/team/).

Sometimes repeated signs suggest that a pupil may benefit from a specialist assessment. It is then raised carefully with the family and the school, and a support plan follows under professional supervision. Some situations are beyond what a guardian can resolve alone, and the family is told so at once.

## Questions and answers

### Do I need a guardian to study in the UK?

If you are under 18 and have come from abroad, normally yes: the school expects a named guardian in the country, and that includes a 16-year-old. The details depend on the school and are settled before term starts.

### Can a relative act as guardian?

The school decides whom it accepts in the role. It is best to ask before the place is confirmed, not afterwards.

### Can we take guardianship without using ALBION for admissions?

As a rule, no. The service is intended for pupils whose admission the ALBION team handled, and places are few. If your circumstances are unusual, we reply individually.

### How does guardianship relate to choosing a school?

It is worth considering while the shortlist is still being drawn. If a school has not yet been chosen, [entrance exam preparation](/private-schools/entrance-exams/) and the admissions steps can be arranged so that support carries on once term begins.
