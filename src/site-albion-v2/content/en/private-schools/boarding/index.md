# Boarding Schools in the UK: Admissions Support from One Team

A boarding application involves three jobs, usually done by three different people. Someone chooses the schools, someone prepares the child for the tests, and once an offer has arrived someone acts as guardian. ALBION does all three from Oxford. One consultant stays with your family from the first conversation to the first term, and ALBION teachers assess and tutor your child.

## Is boarding the right choice?

Boarding does not suit a child who does not want to go, and it will not solve difficulties at home. You may have come across the phrase "boarding school syndrome", which describes problems reported by some adults who boarded young. We take it seriously, so the first consultation covers age, temperament, independence and the boarding pattern. If the honest advice is a [day school](/private-schools/day-schools/) or another year at home, we will say so.

## Entry points and boarding patterns

British boarding schools admit at the same points as other independent schools: 7+ or 8+ into a prep school, 11+ or 13+ into a senior school, 16+ into the sixth form. The procedure differs at each, from a school's own assessment at 7+ to subject tests for A-Level and, for pupils educated outside the UK, UKiset at 16+. Our page on [11+, 13+ and 16+ entrance exams](/private-schools/entrance-exams/) covers the tests one by one.

There are three patterns: full boarding, weekly boarding (home at weekends) and flexi boarding (individual nights). Families overseas mostly use full boarding. The other two work best when you live in the UK.

## Boarding schools in England, and in London

Boarding schools in England are spread across the whole country, so the region matters chiefly for travel: how far the child will be from home, and how easily a guardian can reach the school. Boarding schools in London itself are few, and many described as London schools sit in the counties around the city.

## From the first form to the offers

**1. Application form and first consultation.** You tell us the child's age and year group, the entry year, the current school, where the family lives and a rough budget. The consultation then covers goals, timing and what you expect from boarding.

**2. Academic diagnostic.** English is checked first, so that language does not distort the subject results. The pupil then has one-to-one online lessons with British teachers and sits mock tests under exam conditions. For sixth-form entry the diagnostic covers four A-Level subjects chosen by the pupil, with a detailed report on each.

**3. The shortlist.** Consultants draw up an initial list of up to ten schools, each with reasons, and explain the application process and entry requirements. We suggest narrowing it to five schools to apply to.

**4. Preparation.** Tutors close gaps in the subjects and practise the formats the chosen schools use, online tests included. Nearer the dates they run mock interviews with feedback. The diagnostic sets the intensity of lessons, and every quarter you receive a note on progress and readiness.

**5. Applications.** The consultant helps with forms and letters, watches formal deadlines and informal conventions, and negotiates with schools on your behalf. Once you have registered and paid the registration fee, the school invites your child to sit exams and interviews. The decision is the school's.

**6. Offers and the choice.** We compare the offers with you and, if needed, widen or change the list. After enrolment, pupils whose admission we managed can move on to guardianship: contact with the school, academic monitoring and holidays.

The consultant who builds the shortlist also sees how preparation is going, so the list or the timetable can change when the picture does. ## No commission from schools

Some consultancies charge families nothing because schools fund them, and an adviser paid that way will naturally work from the schools that pay. ALBION is paid by the family and takes no commission from schools. The shortlist is therefore built from the child's profile and the schools' requirements alone, and it can include a strong school that few overseas families have heard of.

## What are the best boarding schools in the UK?

There is no single answer, because "best" depends on the child. League tables mostly show how selective a school is on entry, which says as much about who is admitted as about what the school adds. A good boarding school for one child can be the wrong place for another. Lists of the top UK boarding schools say nothing about pastoral care, the boarding pattern or how a particular child will cope.

We do not publish a ranking or name schools. The shortlist of up to ten starts from the academic assessment and the child's temperament, then looks at how competitive entry is for that child.

## Boarding fees and our fees

Published market figures for fees at private boarding schools, per term, are £9,500–15,000 for pupils aged 8–13 and £13,000–18,000 for pupils aged 11–18, over three terms a year. A registration fee, exam fees and the cost of travel and holidays come on top.

What we charge is settled after the form and the first consultation, and it rises with the length of the shortlist and the hours of preparation. ## Competition, timing and what can go wrong

Entry is competitive, and no school is a safe bet. Across past admissions, 96% of ALBION pupils have ended up at the school they chose. That is a record, not a promise: the decision always sits with the school.

In one family a child's GCSE results meant that continuing to A-Level at the boarding school was no longer possible, with less than two weeks to the start of the school year. Rather than look only for places straight onto A-Level, we found a small, supportive school offering a one-year GCSE resit programme. The grades rose substantially, the pupil went on to A-Level, did well, and is now finishing university. That is one outcome, not an average, but a poor start is not always a closed door.

Plans can also shift once the first offers are in. A girl applying for Year 9 held two offers, while the school her family had ranked highest offered only a waiting-list place. We advised accepting the confirmed offer and kept in regular touch with the first-choice school. With about ten days to go before term, a place there opened up. The possibility had been discussed in advance, so the family decided quickly, knowing that withdrawing late would cost a term's fees. She has settled well. ## Questions and answers

### How old does my child have to be to board?

Places exist from seven, but most boarders arrive later: traditionally girls from 11 and boys from 13. Boarding for A-Level or the IB, from 16, is becoming more common. The younger the child, the closer you should look at pastoral care.

### Is boarding just a day school with a bed?

No. The school is responsible for daily life as well as lessons: housing, meals and routine. Compare pastoral systems, school size and the boarding community, not only exam results.

### Do we need a guardian?

Yes. If the parents live abroad, a British school will ask for an official guardian for any child under 18. It is better to find one before an offer arrives than after: that way you know, when you choose the school, who will be near your child in the holidays.

### What happens if my child is not offered a place?

That is why the list holds several schools at different levels of competition. If the result is not what you hoped, consultants revisit the list and look at other entry points and timing. Sometimes waiting a year is the better route.

### When should we start?

With the academic assessment. It shows how much time this particular child needs, and the shortlist and the entry year follow from that. At 16+ places can still be found on a short timetable, but the strategy is different.

### Can a child with learning differences board?

Schools vary a great deal: some specialise in this, others have dedicated departments. Where a specialist assessment is needed, ALBION arranges it and uses the results when drawing up the list. In one case it led to extra time in exams.

### Co-educational or single-sex?

Both produce strong results. The choice depends on temperament and the school's culture, and we show options of each type when they fit the profile.

### Are there state boarding schools in the UK?

Yes. In state boarding schools in the UK the state pays for the teaching and families pay for the boarding. Places are generally limited to children with the right to live in the UK, so for most families overseas the practical choice is among independent schools. If you hold UK status, say so in the form: it changes which options are open.

### What are boarding colleges?

The term is used loosely, but it usually means a small independent college that takes older pupils, often from 16, for A-Level or a GCSE resit, and houses them. It can suit a teenager with a clear subject plan or one who needs a second attempt at GCSE, like the one-year resit in the case above.

### What counts as an international boarding school?

The phrase covers two things: British boarding schools with pupils from many countries, and schools abroad that follow a British or international curriculum. ALBION works with schools in the UK. For a pupil educated outside it, the usual extras are an English check, UKiset at 16+ for some schools and a guardian.
