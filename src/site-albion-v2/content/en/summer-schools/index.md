# Summer Schools and Camps in the UK

A British summer can be a week of day camp near home, a fortnight on a Cambridge campus, or two weeks in university halls in London with work experience in a chosen field. ALBION advisers match the programme to what your child wants from the summer and what you are prepared to spend. We recommend only programmes for which we hold provider materials, covering ages 6 to 18.

## Day camps, ages 6 to 12

The camps run with Ultimate Activity Camps in independent schools in Oxford, London, Reading and Berkshire. A week runs Monday to Friday, and a course lasts from one to seven weeks. Each child has two 50-minute English lessons a day in groups of up to 12, then chooses from more than 40 activities. The provider says that around 85% of the children are native English speakers, though the share varies by site.

The 2024 materials list Chandlings and Headington in Oxford, Holy Cross in Kingston, Fulham Cross and Sutton High School in London, Crosfields in Reading and LVS Ascot in Berkshire. A teaching week costs £595. Parents who stay nearby can arrange language courses and leisure for themselves as an option. The Oxford sites have their own page on [Oxford summer schools](/summer-schools/oxford/).

## Residential programmes for teenagers

### Dulwich College, ages 12–14

Career Discovery was added to the college's programme for summer 2026: two weeks for children who have not yet settled on a direction. In small groups they work through industry simulations in medicine, finance, business, computing, artificial intelligence, robotics and engineering, and spend two days in workshops on interview preparation and career skills. English level B1+ is required. In 2026 the courses started on 6 and 20 July.

The fee covers 25 hours a week of simulations and lessons, a social programme, London excursions, a graduation ceremony, a reference letter, a certificate and insurance. The residential option means a place in the college boarding houses on the London campus, with meals and pastoral support. It costs £5,495, or £5,695 with a private bathroom.

### Earlscliffe, ages 13–17

Earlscliffe is an independent college for older students in Folkestone, Kent, 54 minutes by road from London according to the provider. In summer it becomes an international school in partnership with SBC and Dukes Education. Lessons run for 20 hours a week in classes of up to 12, and evenings and weekends are filled with excursions and a social programme.

The two-week subject courses cost £3,795: Science and Technology (sustainable future), Future Business Leaders, University Preparation (which includes practical guidance on UK applications) and Business: Mini-MBA. Think Global, which pairs English with international affairs and current events, costs £3,495 and is open from level A2+. The others require B1+. A separate spring General English course takes children aged 8 to 17 and costs £1,790 for one week or £2,685 for two.

### Dukes Cambridge, ages 14–17

The course is held in three Dukes Education schools: Cardiff Sixth Form College, St Andrew's Cambridge and Sancton Wood School. The faculties are art and design, English Pathway, medicine (developed with Medic Portal), sciences and technology, psychology and business. Art and design and English Pathway accept any level of English. The rest need B1+.

In 2026 the two-week sessions started on 29 June, 13 July and 27 July. Two weeks cost £4,395 on English Pathway and £4,895 on the other faculties, and four weeks £8,790 and £9,790. The fee includes 20 hours of teaching a week, accommodation, meals, excursions, insurance and language support. Each student leaves with a certificate and an academic report.

### InvestIN, ages 15–18

InvestIN runs one or two weeks in July and August: theory, practice alongside industry specialists, and visits to real workplaces. There are 15 specialisms, and not every one runs on every date. In 2025, medicine, law, investment and engineering ran on all three starts (7 July, 28 July and 18 August). Psychology and entrepreneurship ran in July only, architecture on 7 July alone, and art, computer science, dentistry, forensic science and several others on 28 July only.

One week costs £2,150 without accommodation and £3,550 on campus. Two weeks cost £3,150 and £6,150, and the premium fortnight £4,250 and £7,250. The provider's 2025 materials offered ALBION clients a discount of £150; whether it applies in the new season is confirmed with the provider. Residential places are en-suite single rooms in university halls, with breakfast, dinner, evening and weekend activities and 24/7 safeguarding and support from a named student support lead (PAL). A Heathrow transfer is £300 return. The official work-experience certificate (the Level 3 Award, which the provider says earns UCAS points) is included in premium and costs a further £375 on the standard fortnight. For a teenager still choosing a field, the course gives [career guidance](/career-guidance/) something concrete to work with.

## Choosing between them

Most families start from one of four aims. Speaking practice points to a day camp full of native speakers or, for an older child, a residential course that pairs lessons with a subject. A first look at a profession points to simulations and placements. University preparation points to a subject faculty on a school or college campus. A trial of boarding life needs nothing more than a fortnight away.

After the aim come the limits: age, minimum level of English, residential or day format, dates and budget. We work with providers tested over the years and well reviewed by students and parents, and a recommendation reaches you only when all of those line up.

## How a summer fits the longer plan

A fortnight away is useful evidence if you are weighing [boarding schools](/private-schools/boarding/). It shows how a child handles routine, a teaching language not their own and life in a group. To see where a child stands against UK peers, our teachers run an [academic assessment](/assessment/) online.

For a student who may apply to university, the programmes worth choosing leave something to discuss later: a placement, a project, a reference letter. Which of them belong in a profile is decided with you as part of [university admissions](/university-admissions/) planning. If English is below a course's level, [language preparation](/language-preparation/) comes first.

## Questions and answers

### How young can a child be for a UK summer camp?

Six, for the day camps in our materials. Residential programmes start higher, at 12 for Dulwich College and 15 for InvestIN. Earlscliffe's spring course takes children from 8.

### What level of English does my child need?

It varies by course. Earlscliffe's spring course starts at A1+, Think Global at A2+, and most subject courses ask for B1+. Dukes Cambridge accepts any level on art and design and English Pathway. InvestIN is designed for teenagers with good English.

### What will a summer cost in total?

From £595 for a week of day camp to £7,250 for the InvestIN premium fortnight with accommodation. Apart from the premium option, the two-week residential courses in the table fall between £3,495 and £6,150. The final sum is confirmed once a programme has been chosen, for the current season.

### Does my child need a visa?

That depends on nationality, length of stay and programme. ALBION helps with UK Visitor, Child Student and Student visa applications, and your family's position is checked before anything is booked.

### Who looks after my child outside lessons?

Provider staff. At InvestIN that means 24/7 safeguarding and support with a named lead, at Dulwich College pastoral support in the boarding house, and at Earlscliffe adult-supervised evening activities. Which format suits depends on your child's age and on whether they have spent time away from home before.

### Will a summer programme help a university application?

It adds to a profile and does not replace grades. At InvestIN the Level 3 Award comes with an employer reference letter and, per the provider, UCAS points. Dulwich College gives a reference letter and certificate, Dukes and Earlscliffe a certificate and academic report.

### How early should we apply?

Early enough to compare options. Places on each date are limited, and some InvestIN specialisms run on one date only.
