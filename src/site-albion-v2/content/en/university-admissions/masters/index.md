# Master's Admissions in the UK and US

Nobody coordinates a master's application on your behalf. There is no UCAS: each university runs its own portal, sets its own document list and closes on its own dates. The outcome depends largely on which programmes you choose and how clearly the file argues for them. ALBION works on both, from a shortlist of up to three programmes to interview preparation and the final decision.

## Choosing the programmes

We begin with your transcript, your subject background and the reason for doing the degree: a career move, a change of field, a doctorate. Module lists come next, together with who teaches, what the entry requirements are and how each programme is assessed. The result is a shortlist of up to three.

The limit is deliberate. Every application needs its own positioning, and ten files that differ only in the name of the university cannot have it. Rankings do not settle the matter either. The question is whether this programme suits this person.

## Building the file

Positioning comes first. Which parts of your record support the programme, where are the gaps, and what closes them: a course, a test, a project, work experience? If you are changing field, the aim is for the move to read as a logical next step.

The academic CV leads with study, research and project work, and with publications where they exist, which makes it a different document from a business résumé. Each programme gets its own personal statement. For a master's it is closer to a statement of purpose: why this programme, what your degree has prepared you for, what you will do with the result. If you would like to see the undergraduate version first, it is explained on our [personal statement page](/university-admissions/personal-statement/).

References need planning. Whom to approach, and what each letter should cover so that together they add up rather than overlap. If a programme asks for a writing sample or a portfolio, we prepare that at the same stage.

## Deadlines, submission and interviews

Some programmes assess applications on a rolling basis, so places can be gone before the published deadline. Others admit in rounds. The working rule is to plan back from the earliest programme on your list, not from a calendar built around UCAS.

We coordinate the submissions and track each date. Where a programme interviews, you rehearse in a format close to the real one and go through your answers with a consultant.

## Taught or research degree

The type of degree shapes the whole file. Taught programmes (MSc, MA) revolve around modules and an exam or dissertation. Research degrees (MRes, MPhil) are built on a topic and a supervisor willing to take it on, so the topic has to be settled earlier and argued harder.

## Who we work with

Four kinds of applicant come to us for a master's. Some are bachelor's graduates moving up a level, and some already hold a postgraduate qualification and want a narrower specialism. Others are changing academic field altogether, and the rest are preparing for a research route into an MPhil or PhD.

We work across disciplines, from politics to quantum physics, with universities in the UK and the US. Experienced managers looking at an MBA or a management master's follow a separate route, described below.

## Tests and English

Some programmes ask for GMAT or GRE scores, most often in business and economics. International applicants usually also need to evidence their English, typically with IELTS. Our tutors prepare candidates for all three, and the test date is set so that a score arrives before your first deadline. More on [admissions test preparation](/university-admissions/admissions-tests/) and [English language preparation](/language-preparation/).

## US programmes, research degrees and MBAs

US master's applications run on different rules: separate tests, a distinct essay for each university and the question of financial aid. We cover them on our [US admissions page](/university-admissions/usa/). For Continental Europe, see [European university admissions](/university-admissions/europe/).

PhD admission turns on three things: risk, fit with the research area and the likelihood of funding. A committee wants to know whether your research path is coherent, whether a supervisor can realistically be found for the topic, and whether years of investment in you are justified. The preparation is different, and it is set out on the [PhD admissions page](/university-admissions/phd/).

MBA, Executive MBA and management master's applications are judged on managerial experience and the make-up of the cohort. Our [MBA admissions service](/executive-education/mba/) handles them. ALBION's co-founder, Anfisa Bashkirova, is a graduate of Saïd Business School, Oxford, and 10+ adult clients completed Saïd programmes with us in the past year.

## Fees and timing

Tuition is set by the university and published on its website. What we charge rises with the number of programmes on the shortlist and the volume of documents each one needs, and we give the figure after the initial consultation. Scholarships such as Chevening and GREAT carry their own conditions, deadlines and nationality rules. We check whether they apply before you plan around one.

Start with the programmes, not the writing. A statement drafted before you know where you are applying will read as generic.

## Where our service stops

ALBION cannot promise an offer, because the decision is the university's. If your profile falls short of the programmes you had in mind, you will hear it from us. Alternatives follow: a different programme, a year to strengthen the profile, a further course or a test.

## Questions and answers

### When should I start?

As soon as you know your target start year, and before you draft anything. Choosing programmes comes first, and rolling programmes can fill early.

### Will a lower undergraduate grade rule me out?

Not automatically. Each programme sets its own threshold. We check the formal minimum and look at what in your record offsets a weaker grade: relevant work, research, a test score or further study.

### Do I need a research proposal?

For research degrees (MRes, MPhil, PhD) often yes, and for taught programmes generally not. The programme page states what is required.

### Can I move into a subject I did not study?

Often, though it depends on the programme. Some accept related backgrounds, others ask for specific undergraduate subjects. We look at how the gap can be bridged.

### Can I apply to more than three programmes?

Within one ALBION engagement the shortlist stops at three, and we do not run more. Each of the three gets its own statement and its own positioning.

### Are you independent of the universities?

Yes. We take no commission from them, so the shortlist is built around your profile and your goal and not around anyone's partnership list.

### How is a master's different from an MBA?

An academic master's is built around a subject, an MBA around managerial experience and a career transition. We handle them as separate services.

The form asks about your goal, previous education, intended start year and a rough budget. An initial consultation follows.
