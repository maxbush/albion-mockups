# Oxbridge Admissions Support

The two Oxbridge universities ask for more than other UK universities do: a single choice between them, an earlier deadline, an admissions test on many courses and an interview in December. Our Oxbridge application support is built around a personal mentor, who stays with the application from the first assessment of competitiveness to the decision on offers. It suits students with a strong academic profile, and parents who want an unvarnished view of how that profile compares.

## Who reads the application

Applications arrive through UCAS, but they are read by tutors at a college and in a faculty. Oxford has 32 undergraduate colleges, and every one runs its own admissions panel, open days and calendar. The choice of college decides whose panel considers the file.

Four things are weighed together: actual and predicted grades, the personal statement, the admissions test where the course has one, and the interview. Because of that, the first step is to look at the whole profile and say where it would stand beside British applicants of the same age.

## Dates, and what to do with the other choices

The usual deadline is 15 October, three months before the main UCAS deadline. A required admissions test has its own registration, which should be checked early. Interviews take place in December.

Oxbridge applications still leave other places on the UCAS form, and they carry real weight. If no interview invitation arrives, a considered fallback should already be in place. The [admissions overview](/university-admissions/) explains how those applications work, and the [UCAS support page](/university-admissions/ucas/) covers submission and deadlines.

## Tests and interviews

Oxford admissions tests, like Cambridge's, are tied to the course, and which courses need a test depends on the university and the year of entry. The table is a guide only, and the requirements for your year are checked on the college or faculty website before any plan is made.

| Test | Typically used for |
|---|---|
| LNAT | Law |
| MAT | Mathematics at Oxford |
| TSA and TARA | Thinking and reasoning tests on selected Oxford and Cambridge courses |

Our page on [admissions tests](/university-admissions/admissions-tests/) covers each one in more detail.

The Oxford interview, like the Cambridge one, is a conversation about your subject, and what it tests is academic argument and depth of knowledge. Preparation takes the form of mock sessions, and after each one the mentor goes through the answers with you. Where the gap is in the subject itself, a tutor works on it.

## Open days and college visits

Colleges and faculties keep separate calendars, and any family can make only a limited number of visits. A plan drawn up in advance settles which colleges suit the subject, when admissions staff are on hand and where mock interviews are demonstrated.

ALBION prepares the strategy and schedule for each visit and can go with you on the day. The strategy costs £350 per visit and the accompaniment another £350 per visit. ## Questions and answers

### Can you apply to Oxford and Cambridge in the same year?

No. You may apply to one of the two. The remaining choices on the UCAS form go to other universities, so the decision between them rests on the subject, the course format and the tests involved.

### How do I choose a college?

By subject, by how the college admits for your course, and by what you saw on an open day. The reasoning behind the choice is part of stage 1.

### What if I am not invited to interview?

By then the other UCAS choices have been prepared as part of the fallback strategy. At stage 4 we go through the replies and decide which of the available offers to accept.

### Is interview preparation covered by the £9,500?

Preparation for the interview, with mock sessions and a review, is part of the programme. Lessons with a subject tutor are billed separately at the hourly rate, and the number of hours depends on what needs strengthening.

### When are Oxford interviews held?

In December, after the 15 October deadline. Each college keeps its own calendar and sends its own invitations, so the exact dates for your course are taken from the college website once the application is in. Test registration, mock sessions and the fallback choices are all planned back from that month.

### How to get into Cambridge University: where do I start?

The Cambridge application goes through UCAS by the Oxbridge deadline of 15 October, and you apply to Cambridge or Oxford in a given year, not both. Where the course asks for a test you sit it, and in December there is an interview. A college reads the file and weighs grades, personal statement, test and interview together, so the first step is an honest look at the whole profile. That is what the Oxbridge consultation with a written report is for.

### How to get into Oxford University: is the route different from Cambridge?

The steps match those for Cambridge, and the differences lie in the details. Oxford sets the tests by course, for example MAT for mathematics and LNAT for law, and an applicant can name a college or leave the choice to the university. We compare both options against your profile in the consultation.

### Where can I find mathematics admissions test past papers?

Oxford publishes past papers for the MAT, and they are the standard practice material. A tutor uses them for a timed diagnostic, goes through each error with you and adjusts the plan. The format for your year is confirmed against the university's own information.

### What does it cost to study at Oxford itself?

Tuition fees are set by the university and depend on the course and on your fee status. ALBION publishes only the cost of its own work. Current university fees should be checked on the university's website, and at the consultation we set them against your family's plans.

### Can international students apply?

Yes, international applicants apply through UCAS alongside everyone else. Funding and visa questions depend on status and country of residence, and are best checked early against the university's own information.
