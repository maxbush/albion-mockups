# University Admissions Test Preparation: LNAT, TMUA, MAT, UCAT, GMAT and GRE

Strong grades do not always settle an application. University admissions tests in the UK are tied to particular courses. For law, mathematics, economics, medicine and a number of others, the university asks for a separate test, and at some programmes the score helps decide who is invited to interview. ALBION prepares applicants for LNAT, TARA, TMUA, MAT and TSA, for SAT, and for GMAT and GRE at postgraduate level. Our tutors teach the test. Our consultants decide which test is needed, by when, and how it fits the rest of the file.

## Working back from the deadline

The UCAS main deadline is 15 January, and for Oxford and Cambridge it is 15 October. Tests keep calendars of their own, and registration often closes before the application does, so the plan starts from the test dates and not from the submission date. The [Oxbridge page](/university-admissions/oxbridge/) covers that cycle in detail, and the [university admissions overview](/university-admissions/) explains the wider timeline.

Courses and universities are normally chosen before anyone knows which tests will be required. In an ALBION admissions programme the question of tests comes up while courses are being chosen, not once the shortlist is final. For Oxford and Cambridge, consultants coordinate test preparation with the rest of the candidate's positioning, so the tutor receives a clear brief: which test, by what date, to what standard.

A weak score does not necessarily end an application. If it falls below what a course needs, the consultant reviews the list of universities and the fallback options, sometimes shifting weight towards universities where the test counts for less.

## The tests and who sits them

Test requirements change from year to year, so treat what follows as a map and not a rulebook. For your own programme we check the university's current requirements before any plan is made.

### Law and reasoning: LNAT, TARA and TSA

LNAT is used for law at Oxford and a number of other UK universities. It has two parts, verbal reasoning and an argumentative essay, and each calls for a different technique. LNAT preparation therefore treats them separately, and an LNAT tutor works on the verbal section and the essay in turn.

The TARA admissions test and the TSA are the thinking and reasoning tests used by Oxford and Cambridge on some courses. Their names, and the courses that use them, have changed in recent years, so we confirm them against the college or faculty for your year of entry. TARA test preparation follows the same pattern as the rest: past papers under time, then a review of every error.

### Mathematics: TMUA and MAT

TMUA is used for mathematics, economics and computer science at some universities and tests mathematical reasoning built on the school syllabus. MAT is Oxford's mathematics test, with problems that sit well outside routine exam practice. TMUA preparation, like MAT preparation for Oxford, rests on the A-Level syllabus the tests grow out of. A TMUA tutor is a mathematics specialist who builds from that syllabus.

### Medicine: UCAT

UCAT is for medicine and dentistry and assesses cognitive ability and situational judgement. The older BMAT, once used by medical schools, has been discontinued. If you are looking for a UCAT preparation course, ALBION's route is individual tutoring, arranged case by case: tutor availability for this test is confirmed at the initial consultation.

### US and postgraduate: SAT, GMAT and GRE

SAT is one of the tests used for US undergraduate admission, and it sits within our [US admissions support](/university-admissions/usa/). SAT preparation is individual tutoring planned with the rest of the US application, not a ready-made course. GMAT and GRE serve business schools and master's programmes in the UK and the US.

### English language: IELTS

International applicants are usually asked to show their English, often with IELTS, and the score a course needs is set by the university. IELTS preparation for university entry runs through our [language preparation](/language-preparation/) service, and the sitting is planned back from the application deadline like any other test.

For every test above, registration and sitting dates are deliberately left off this page. Each test has its own calendar, which moves from one cycle to the next, and we check it against the test provider when drawing up your plan.

## Choosing between GMAT and GRE

For a business school or a master's, the question is often which test to take rather than whether. Many business schools accept both, so the answer comes from the programmes on your list.

Read what each programme accepts. Take a timed diagnostic of each test and compare how you actually perform. Then set the sitting date back from your first application deadline, leaving room for a retake. Programmes are chosen first, and our work on [master's admissions](/university-admissions/masters/) and [MBA admissions](/executive-education/mba/) starts from that point.

## How the tutoring works

The first session is a diagnostic. You work through past papers under time pressure, and the tutor goes through the result to see where marks are lost: speed, a particular question type, or the wording of the questions. Our [academic assessment](/assessment/) rests on the same principle, and it also draws on past papers.

From there the tutor works on the specific test, not on "reasoning" in general. Timed practice papers follow, then a review of each error, and the plan shifts with your results.

## What preparation cannot do

We do not promise scores. How far a candidate gets depends on where they start, how long there is and how much they practise between lessons. A tutor does not sit the test for you or replace the other parts of the application: grades, personal statement and interview. A late start narrows the options as well. In a short window you can close gaps in format and technique, but not gaps in the school syllabus.

## Questions and answers

### When should I start preparing for LNAT, TMUA or MAT?

Well ahead of the application. The date depends on your test and your starting level, and the first step is a diagnostic on past papers.

### Does every UK university require a test?

No. Tests are tied to particular courses, most often at Oxford and Cambridge, in law, mathematics, economics and medicine. Many other courses are decided on grades and the personal statement.

### Can I prepare on my own?

Many applicants do. A tutor helps when progress has stalled, time is short, or it is hard to see for yourself where marks are going.

### How much does an hour of test preparation cost?

From £110 with a specialist tutor and from £150 with a senior specialist. An academic tutor (from £65) is generally not intended for specialist admissions preparation. The total follows from the number of lessons, which we set after the diagnostic.

### Can I book test tutoring without the admissions programme?

Yes. Tutoring is billed separately and is not tied to the programme. When it runs alongside admissions support, our consultants coordinate it with your application strategy.

### Do I need the GMAT or GRE for an MBA?

Schools differ. Some require a score, others make it optional or accept an alternative. We check this when we choose programmes with you.
