# About ALBION

ALBION is an Oxford consultancy that has advised families on British schools and universities since 2010, and has guided adult clients towards postgraduate and executive programmes. Its motto is "More than consultancy". The evidence for it is practical: who pays for the advice, how the team is organised, and what a family never has to explain.

## Working from Oxford

Oxford is a city where academic tradition is part of ordinary life and scientific breakthroughs sit alongside eccentric student customs. That setting shapes the firm's habits: decisions are considered, and education is treated as a long project that deserves real investment. The team visits schools, boarding houses and universities across the UK, so what it says about a school comes from recent first-hand knowledge.

## A shared language

Many clients are international families, and the team speaks their language in both senses. Its members studied in Britain themselves, and were parents who wanted the best for their children and had to understand an unfamiliar system from scratch. A family rarely has to explain what a guardian does or what preparing for 13+ involves.

## Advice and teaching under one roof

Families often meet these two roles in different companies: one writes the admissions plan, another teaches the child. At ALBION they work side by side. If the shortlist or the timetable changes, the tutor preparing a child for ISEB, UKiset, CAT4, LNAT or TARA hears at once, and the lessons follow the plan. The 150+ tutors work across time zones, and each one has been chosen personally by Jonathan Mintram.

Guardianship belongs to the same structure. It is offered mainly to pupils whose admission the team handled, and places are limited because the consultant stays closely involved. We do not market it as a mass service, and the [guardianship page](/private-schools/guardianship/) has the detail.

## Who pays for the advice

Families pay us. Schools and universities do not, and no commission comes from either. A shortlist is therefore built around the pupil or candidate: where the application is competitive, and where the person is likely to do well once there.

The same logic governs the choice of course. A university is a tool for a career and a life, not a destination, so each course is judged by where the student is likely to stand in ten years rather than three or four. One limit follows from this. We advise, and the offer of a place is made by the school or the university.

## How a case is approached

Analysis comes first, then planning. For a school application the team looks at the child's academic history, level of English, current results, interests and any learning difficulties that need attention. For a university application it looks at subject combination, exam results and the likely direction of a career. A plan follows: selection, positioning, preparation, submission and fallback options.

Adults heading for executive programmes have a method of their own, which tests how a professional profile fits a particular programme. Anfisa Bashkirova designed it. She is a Saïd Business School graduate and takes part in candidate recruitment as an alumni representative.

## The whole span of British education

The mission is to stay with a family through all of it. The relationship can begin early, with a governess or tutor and a preparatory school, and it can end with an adult on an [MBA or executive programme](/executive-education/). In between lie [private schools](/private-schools/), [university admissions](/university-admissions/) including Oxbridge, master's and doctoral study, and [tutoring](/tutors/).
