# Easter Revision Courses for A-Level and GCSE

Mock results usually arrive with a list of things that went wrong, and the Easter holiday is the last long stretch in which to deal with them before the May and June papers. With lessons paused, there is time to find where marks are leaking and to repair it. ALBION tutors spend that time on exam technique: timed past papers, marking against the mark scheme and a close look at every error.

## Where marks are lost

British papers award marks against criteria, so knowing the material accounts for only part of the result. In maths, the method earns marks as well as the answer, which means working should be set out so that it still scores if the last line goes wrong. In English and the humanities, assessment objectives decide what a question tests and what a response has to show. In the sciences and economics, command words such as explain, evaluate and discuss call for different depths of answer.

Most of a tutor's time goes here. A topic the student already understands is not taught again. The task is to turn that understanding into marks.

## Inside a course

The tutor begins with recent mock papers and school work, and picks the topics likely to return the most marks. Full past papers from the student's own exam board, whether AQA, Edexcel, OCR or another, are then sat under timed conditions and marked against the published scheme.

Every lost mark is given a cause: a gap in knowledge, a slip in calculation, a misread command word, time pressure or a loosely built answer. The cause shows what needs teaching and what only needs practice. Weak topics return in shorter tasks until the result holds.

## Who it suits, and who should wait

A course suits a student who knows the content but sees gaps in mock results: answers that run out of time, marks lost on wording though the topic is understood, errors that repeat from paper to paper. It also suits a candidate sitting British exams outside a UK school who wants to test preparation against exam board format.

It suits a student less when the basics of a subject are missing and the exam is weeks away. A holiday course cannot rebuild a syllabus. After the diagnosis we say so, and suggest either regular lessons with an [A-Level tutor](/tutors/a-level/) or a [GCSE tutor](/tutors/gcse/), or a narrower aim for the course.

## Subjects, format and dates

Subjects and exam boards are confirmed when a place is booked, and the final list depends on which tutors are available. Courses are online, with sessions set around the student's time zone.

Dates are published once they are confirmed, and we do not quote provisional ones. A family that needs a course in the coming holidays can start with the [short form](/apply/), which asks for year group, subjects, exam board and preferred timing. We reply with the options that are open.

## Fees

## What we do not promise

We cannot promise a grade. A course improves the odds through technique and error analysis, but it does not stand in for a year of work. If the diagnosis shows that a target is out of reach, we say so before the course begins. ## Questions and answers

### When should we apply?

Early. Diagnosis and tutor matching take time, and the Easter break is short. The form can be completed before dates are published, and we contact the family once a course is confirmed.

### Does it cover both GCSE and A-Level?

Yes, though the work differs. GCSE leans towards precise wording and secure basics, A-Level towards depth and argument. The tutor is matched to subject and level.

### Do we need to know the exam board?

It helps, and it is normally printed in school documents. If you are unsure, we can identify it from the most recent mock paper.

### Is it one-to-one or in a group?

That depends on the course, and we confirm the details with the programme. One-to-one work with a tutor is possible in any holiday.

### Can families outside the UK take part?

Yes, if the student is sitting British exams, for instance IGCSE or A-Level at an international school. Lessons are online. Term dates abroad may not match the British ones, and we discuss that at booking.

### How many hours will a student need?

The diagnosis decides, since it depends on the subjects and the size of the gaps. We give the hours together with the programme and do not quote a range in advance.
