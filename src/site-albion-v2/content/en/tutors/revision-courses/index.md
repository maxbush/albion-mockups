# Revision Courses for GCSE, A-Level and IB

A revision course is a short, concentrated block of work before an exam. It suits a student who has already covered the syllabus and now needs to turn knowledge into marks: weak topics repaired, papers practised against the clock, mistakes understood rather than repeated. ALBION tutors run these courses in school holidays and in the weeks before the exam series, for students at the final stage of GCSE, A-Level or the IB Diploma and for those preparing a resit.

## Three points in the school year

The main GCSE and A-Level series falls in May and June, and the IB session is also in May. That leaves three natural windows. The first is the next break after mock exams, half term or the Christmas holiday, when results are fresh and show what needs repairing. The second is the Easter holiday, the last before the series, and the point around which GCSE Easter revision courses and A-Level Easter revision are built. The third is summer, for a resit or for a difficult subject in the year ahead.

What a course concentrates on depends on the qualification.

| Qualification | Where the weight falls | What a course works on |
|---|---|---|
| GCSE and IGCSE | English, maths and the sciences, where single papers count for a lot | Question wording, mark schemes, accuracy under time |
| A-Level | Three or four subjects that set the conditions of a university offer | Depth of argument, higher-mark questions, board-specific formats |
| IB Diploma | Subject papers, internal assessments and the Extended Essay | Whatever is still open: paper technique, coursework criteria, the EE timetable |

## When a course helps, and when it does not

A course helps a student who knows the content but loses marks. Mock results may show a few weak topics with no room for them in the normal timetable. A student may run out of time on the last questions. A move from the IB to A-Level may have left gaps that must close quickly. For a resit, precision against the mark scheme counts for more than breadth.

It helps much less when the gaps are deep. If the exam is months away, ordinary lessons should come first and a revision block nearer the date. If the exam is two weeks away, a course is still possible, but its aim narrows to technique, answer structure and the usual ways of losing marks. No one can re-teach a subject in that time.

## Revision course or regular tuition?

The two do different jobs. Regular tuition runs alongside school through the year, with a tutor explaining, consolidating and filling gaps as they appear. A revision course starts from the assumption that the material has been taught. It spends its hours on what earns marks: the topics a student still drops marks on, the formats of the questions, the habits that cost time in the exam hall. A course does not replace a year of study.

## Inside a course

It begins with an academic diagnosis. The tutor looks at recent work and mock results, identifies the risks and builds a plan topic by topic. The core of the course is past papers under a timer, a review of every lost mark and repeat attempts on weak topics. Answer format is trained too: for GCSE and A-Level the requirements of the exam board matter, whether AQA, Edexcel or OCR, while IB revision courses follow the criteria of each assessment.

Content, subjects and hours depend on the student's level and on the programme. We agree the details after the short form and the diagnosis, not from a template.

## Online or in Oxford

Online revision courses are the main format, for GCSE and A-Level as much as for the IB, and sessions can be set around the student's day wherever the family lives. In-person lessons in Oxford can be arranged, depending on subject and timing.

When a student is also going through university admissions, a consultant can see how the lessons are going. Predicted grades and offer conditions follow from results, and the application plan may need to follow them.

## Questions and answers

### How early should we start?

The earlier the better, because weak topics need time to be worked through and revisited. A few weeks before the exam can still help, though the result is then limited to technique and error analysis.

### Does a course work if the gaps are serious?

Not always. If the basics are missing, a whole syllabus cannot be rebuilt in a short block. We then suggest regular lessons first and a revision course nearer the exam.

### Are courses one-to-one or in groups?

Our published rates are for one-to-one lessons. Whether a particular course also runs in groups is confirmed when its programme is confirmed.

### Can a course take place in person in Oxford?

By arrangement. Online is the main format, and in-person sessions are discussed after the form, as they depend on subject, tutor and timing.

### Can you promise a particular grade?

No. The outcome depends on the starting point, the time available and the student. We show which topics and question types will repay the time, and we say plainly if a target looks out of reach.

### Are GCSE revision courses online, and do they cover IGCSE?

Online is the main format, and IGCSE is covered. The diagnosis and the past papers follow the chosen exam board, so please give the board and the exam format in the form.

### Is there an A-Level revision course online?

Yes, online is the main format. It begins with the same academic diagnosis, then past papers against the clock and a review of every lost mark, following your exam board. Please name the subject and the board in the form.

### Do you run half term or Christmas revision courses?

Revision courses run in school holidays, and which holiday depends on the programme and the subject. Say in the form which break you have in mind, whether half term, Christmas or Easter, and we confirm what is available. We do not publish dates here.
