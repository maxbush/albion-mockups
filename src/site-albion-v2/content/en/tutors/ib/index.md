# IB Tutors for the Diploma Programme

A student on the IB Diploma is being assessed in several places at once. There are six subjects and the three parts of the core, and coursework is handed in months before the May exams. A weak spot in any one of them lowers the total, and the total sets the conditions of a university offer. ALBION's IB tutoring helps when a subject needs strengthening, when the Extended Essay has stalled, or when the calendar of submissions has to be planned around the courses a student wants to apply for.

## The parts of the Diploma, and where a tutor fits

The Diploma carries a maximum of 45 points: up to 42 from the six subjects and up to 3 from the Extended Essay and TOK together. CAS earns no points, but the Diploma is not awarded without it. Tutoring looks different for each part.

| Part | What it involves | What a tutor does |
|---|---|---|
| Subjects, HL and SL | Six subjects from different groups, three or four at Higher Level and the others at Standard Level | Works through gaps topic by topic and practises exam papers against IB mark schemes |
| Internal assessments | Coursework within a subject, marked by the teacher and sampled by the IB | Helps narrow the topic and method, checks drafts against the criteria and talks through feedback |
| Extended Essay | An independent research paper of up to 4,000 words | Helps frame the research question, plan the work and keep to a timetable, with comments on drafts |
| TOK | An essay and an exhibition on how knowledge is built and tested | Works on how the argument is built, on examples and on the assessment criteria |
| CAS | Creativity, activity, service, recorded as a portfolio of reflections | Keeps the reflections regular and coherent rather than written in one rush |

## Internal assessments

A student can know a subject well and still lose marks on coursework, because the piece does not meet its criteria. In maths the internal work is the Exploration, in economics a portfolio of commentaries, in the sciences an independent investigation. Each is marked against criteria specific to the subject.

A tutor reads a draft the way a moderator would: where a criterion has not been addressed, where the method is unjustified, where the word count has gone to the wrong section. The tutor explains what needs fixing, and the writing stays with the student. The IB treats academic honesty seriously, and so do we.

## Extended Essay, TOK and CAS

With the Extended Essay, the first conversation is about the question. Does the topic suit the subject, is there enough source material, can the argument be made in 4,000 words? A plan then runs back from the date on which the school expects a draft.

TOK rests on construction: a claim, a counterclaim, an example drawn from a real knowledge situation. CAS is light on content and demanding on discipline, since reflections are due across the whole programme and are easy to leave until the last month.

## Higher Level choices and the university list

The Higher Level combination decides which degrees a student can apply for. UK universities publish required subjects and levels course by course, so it is better to check them before the Diploma begins than after. If results in a key subject are flat, the ALBION consultant handling the application can see it and the plan can change in time.

The maths course matters here: Analysis and Approaches and Applications and Interpretation each come at HL and SL, and the choice affects eligibility for some degrees.

## IB maths and science tutors

An IB maths tutor is matched to the course the student is actually taking, whichever of the two versions and levels it is, with the Exploration planned alongside the exam papers. IB physics, chemistry and biology tutors work in the same way: topic by topic through the subject guide, then past papers against IB mark schemes, with the scientific investigation read against its criteria. ALBION tutors teach maths and the natural sciences, and we confirm availability for a particular subject and level when we match, so please name both in the short form.

## Predicted grades and applications

Schools send predicted grades to universities, and an offer arrives as a condition: a points total plus specific grades at Higher Level. Lessons are planned backwards from that target. For a student applying to [Oxford or Cambridge](/university-admissions/oxbridge/), preparation for [admissions tests and interviews](/university-admissions/admissions-tests/) comes on top of subject work, and ALBION matches a specialist tutor for it.

## IB or A-Level

Both qualifications are accepted by UK universities. The IB is broader, with six subjects plus the core. A-Level usually means three subjects studied in greater depth, assessed mainly by final exams. The better fit depends on what the school offers, the student's strengths and the courses in view. Our page on [A-Level tutors](/tutors/a-level/) covers the other route.

## From practice: IB closed overnight

In August before her final year, the IB programme in the student's country was closed overnight, in all schools at once, and her school moved the pupils to A-level. IB and A-level differ greatly in content: economics, which had gone well, suddenly became hard, and we brought in a tutor. She had no GCSE maths grade at all, and resitting without preparation made no sense. Sitting A-level at home was no longer possible, but the family already held a two-year British visa obtained in advance "just in case". On the UCAS deadline day the school's UCAS coordinator returned her application to correct a typo in an exam number, and disappeared. An hour before the deadline we rebuilt the application and submitted her as an independent applicant: we moved everything into a new account and wrote the section a school normally completes, explaining the closure of IB and the missing maths grade. We made it just in time. Three offers came, two of them from Russell Group universities. Two courses fell through because of maths, and one university first refused her without maths and then offered sociology, which turned out closer to her interests. She chose it.

## Questions and answers

### Should my child take IB or A-Level?

We do not rank them. UK universities accept both. The decision usually turns on which one the school teaches, how the student likes to work and which degree is in sight. A student who switches after starting will find the subject content differs, which is covered below.

### How much does an IB tutor cost?

The hourly rate depends on the level of the tutor, and the total on how many hours the subject, the level and the gaps require. See the [tutors overview](/tutors/) for the levels. We name the number of hours only after the assessment.

### Can I hire an IB tutor for the Extended Essay only?

Yes. The EE can be supported separately from subject lessons, with a tutor matched to the field of the essay.

### Can a tutor help with one subject only?

Yes. Lessons are built around the task, whether that is one subject, one piece of internal assessment or the EE. Say which subject and level (HL or SL) in the form.

### How many lessons will we need?

That depends on the assessment: the subject, how long remains before the exams and how large the gap is. We do not promise a fixed-size package in advance.

### Will the tutor write my child's coursework?

No. The tutor explains, reviews and gives feedback. A breach of academic honesty can lead the IB to disqualify a piece of work, and no single mark is worth that risk.

### What if my child is changing from IB to A-Level, or the other way round?

The subject content differs between the two programmes, so the gaps will need closing. A tutor in the relevant subject does that, and an ALBION consultant checks the effect on the application and on predicted grades. For earlier results, there are also [GCSE tutors](/tutors/gcse/).
