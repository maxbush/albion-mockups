# The Cost of Studying in the UK: School and University

The cost of studying in the UK depends on the stage. A private school charges by the term, with a working range from £6,000 for a day school to £18,000 for boarding at ages 11 to 18. At university, a student from England pays no more than £9,790 a year in 2026/27. An international student pays far more: Oxford quotes from £39,620 to £66,580 a year for overseas students, depending on the course. A gap of four times or more shapes the whole family budget.

## Private school

Fees are charged termly, three terms a year. For orientation we use these figures:

| School | Fee per term |
|---|---|
| Boarding, pupils aged 8 to 13 | £9,500 to £15,000 |
| Boarding, pupils aged 11 to 18 | £13,000 to £18,000 |
| Day school | £6,000 to £9,000 |

These are working figures, and the school gives the exact amount. Beyond the termly fee, schools charge separate sums, such as registration and exam fees, and each school's list differs. If the parents live abroad, a child under 18 needs a guardian, which is a separate agreement and a separate cost: see how [guardianship](/private-schools/guardianship/) works.

State schools in England are free for children who are entitled to a place, but they are generally closed to families without UK status. For such families the choice usually comes down to independent schools, and the overall picture is set out on our page about [private schools](/private-schools/).

## University

| Student | Course fee per year | Tuition fee loan |
|---|---|---|
| From England (Home), 2026/27 | Up to £9,790, the government's cap, which is what Oxford charges | A government loan for the full fee is available |
| International (Overseas) | At Oxford from £39,620 to £66,580, depending on the course (stated for 2027/28) | No UK government loan |

Other universities set their own prices; Oxford is used as the example because it publishes a range. Oxford states that fees for international students rise every year and that the rise will not exceed the higher of 6% or CPI inflation. A budget should therefore cover the whole programme with that growth built in, not today's figure.

## Living costs

Oxford estimates living costs in 2026/27 at £1,405 to £2,105 a month for a single student living in college. College accommodation for 175 days costs, in the examples from Oxford's table, from £4,760 (St John's) to £6,298 (Christ Church), with meals counted separately. Costs in other cities differ and should be checked university by university.

## How to work out a budget

For a school, multiply the termly fee by three, then add the charges, guardianship if it is needed, and travel. For a university, add the course fee for every year to the living costs for the same years. At £39,620 a year, for example, the course fee alone for a three-year degree comes to about £119,000, before annual increases and before accommodation. A sum like this shows why the course and the university should be chosen together with the budget, not after it, and it keeps unpleasant surprises out of the third year.

## What the figures leave out

They leave out flights, visas, medical cover and the fees of an adviser. Advice and tutoring are paid for separately, and the published rates are on our [prices](/prices/) page. How an application runs, by date and by UCAS fee, is covered on our page about [UCAS](/university-admissions/ucas/).

## Questions and answers

### How much does a British private school cost?

The working range per term is £6,000 to £9,000 for a day school and £13,000 to £18,000 for boarding at ages 11 to 18. That is three terms a year, plus separate charges. The school gives the exact amount.

### How much does a UK bachelor's degree cost an international student?

It depends on the university and the course. Oxford states a range from £39,620 to £66,580 a year for overseas students, and it rises annually. Living costs come on top.

### Can an international student get a tuition loan?

The government tuition fee loan, as Oxford describes it, is not available to overseas students. Funding is sought through university scholarships and personal means, and the terms are on the university's own site.

The university figures come from the official GOV.UK and Oxford pages and were checked on 15 October 2026.
